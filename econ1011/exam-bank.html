📋 Elasticity calc (Pr-26 Q2b, official model answer) (copy this layout)

📋 "Is demand elastic at the monopoly price?" (S1-26 Q9, lecturer's L13 model answer) (Yes, plus three steps)
Yes. When demand is inelastic (|εD| < 1), marginal revenue is negative (MR < 0). Firms, monopolies included, face non-negative marginal cost (MC ≥ 0). A profit-maximising firm never produces where MR < MC, because then marginal profit is negative and it could raise profit by cutting output and raising price. So the monopolist always operates on the elastic part of demand.

📋 "Which effect dominates?" — war & inflation (S1-26 Q14, lecturer's L13 model answer) (name both shifts, say which is bigger and why, then P)
Heavy oil reliance, confidence not badly hit: the SRAS decrease is large (big upward pressure on prices) and the AD decrease is small (small downward pressure). The SRAS effect dominates, so the price level will likely rise (inflation).
Mostly renewables, confidence seriously damaged (worried about exports, so NX and C/I fall): the SRAS decrease is minimal and the AD decrease is large. The AD effect dominates, so the price level will likely fall (deflation).
📋 "Which effect dominates?" — immigration (S1-25 Q11, our model answer) (4 points: AD effect, SRAS depends on labour force, case 1, case 2)
"Immigration raises AD (they consume) and may raise SRAS (they work and lower labour costs). If most migrants are young and skilled, they join the labour force and add a lot to productive capacity, so the SRAS shift is large and can offset the AD shift. The price level may barely rise or even fall, so immigration is unlikely to be inflationary. If most migrants are dependants outside the labour force, they add to consumption (AD↑) but not to labour supply (SRAS unchanged). That is a pure AD increase, so the price level rises: immigration is inflationary."
📋 Monetary policy after a big supply shock (S2-25 Q8, our model answer) (≤100 words)
Expansionary. Because the SRAS decrease is much larger than the AD increase, the new short-run equilibrium has real output below potential GDP and a higher price level (stagflation). To return output to potential, the central bank should lower the cash rate (or increase the money supply). Lower interest rates raise consumption and investment, shifting AD to the right until it meets SRAS on LRAS. The trade-off is that the price level rises even further, so the bank accepts more inflation to close the output gap.

📋 Dominant strategy — YES (Pr-26 Q5b, official model answer) (swap in names and numbers)
Yes. Country A has a dominant strategy. To see why:
If Country B Restricts: Restrict gives Country A a payoff of 2; Status quo gives Country A a payoff of 1. 2 is better than 1, so "Restrict" is Country A's best response against Country B's "Restrict".
If Country B maintains the Status quo: Restrict gives Country A a payoff of 4; Status quo gives Country A a payoff of 3. 4 is better than 3, so "Restrict" is Country A's best response against Country B's "Status quo".
As "Restrict" is the best response against each and every one of Country B's actions, it is Country A's dominant strategy.
📋 Dominant strategy — NO (Pr-26 Q5c, official model answer) (B = second number)
No. Country B does not have a dominant strategy. To see why:
If Country A Restricts: Restrict gives Country B a payoff of 2; Status quo gives Country B a payoff of 0. 2 is better than 0, so "Restrict" is Country B's best response against Country A's "Restrict".
If Country A maintains the Status quo: Restrict gives Country B a payoff of 0; Status quo gives Country B a payoff of 2. 2 is better than 0, so "Status quo" is Country B's best response against Country A's "Status quo".
As there is no action that can be the best response against each and every one of Country A's actions, Country B does not have a dominant strategy.

📋 "More babies" (Pr-26 Q6d, official model answer) (Yes/No → effect on k* → compare with kGR)
No. Increasing the population growth rate will reduce the steady-state capital-labour ratio. As the current steady-state capital-labour ratio is already below the Golden rule level, the policy will move the steady-state capital-labour ratio further away from the Golden rule level.
📋 Escape the poverty trap (S2-24 Q24, our model answer) (name the curve and its direction)
"Introduce population-control policies (access to birth control, education for women) to lower n. This pivots the break-even investment curve (n+δ)k down, so at the trap's low k, s·f(k) now exceeds break-even investment and k rises. If the shift is large enough, the low (poverty-trap) steady state disappears and the economy grows to the high steady state with higher k and y."
📋 Growth AND higher c_ss (S1-24 Q21, our model answer 🟡) (≤100 words)
"Subsidise R&D and education to raise total factor productivity A. Higher A shifts f(k) and s·f(k) up, so the steady-state k and y both rise. Steady-state consumption c = f(k) − (n+δ)k rises because output rises at every k. Sustained technological progress is the only source of long-run growth in per-capita output, because capital accumulation alone runs into diminishing returns."
