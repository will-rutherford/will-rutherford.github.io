⚙️ The Master Decision Engine

The exam never says "use a paired t-test." It describes a situation and asks a question. The single most valuable exam skill is translating wording → method. This is that translator. Use the trigger table to decode individual phrasings, then the decision tree to lock in the exact inference procedure.

Read the verb and the data structure first. "Construct / determine / find" = compute. "Perform an appropriate test / is there evidence" = full 7-step test. "Comment / discuss / interpret" = words, in the story's context. "State the assumptions" = list them AND judge each against the given plots/table.

B. Decision tree — which inference procedure?

Once you know it's an inference question (CI or test), walk this tree top-down. Answer each question from the wording and the data given.

① What am I being asked to produce?
Complete a table ANOVA table with blanks → go to ANOVA (⑤).
A regression thing slope/prediction/residual/lm() → go to Regression (⑥).
An interval "construct/calculate a CI" → build a CI (②, use CI formula for the case).
A yes/no on a claim "perform a test / is there evidence / test the claim" → run a test (②, use the matching statistic).
② What kind of data / how many samples?
Counts in a table two categorical variables cross-tabulated → χ² test of association (④).
3+ group means comparing means across 3 or more groups → ANOVA (⑤).
Proportion(s) "proportion / percentage / p" → proportion z-procedure (③-P).
Mean(s), numeric continue to ③.
③ Means: how many samples, and are they linked?
One sample compare one group's mean to a fixed number \(\mu_0\).
Test: 1-sample t \(T=\dfrac{\bar X-\mu_0}{S/\sqrt n}\sim t_{n-1}\) (sheet #72). CI: \(\bar X\pm t_{n-1}\,S/\sqrt n\) (#62). 2025 Q3(a).
Two samples · PAIRED same subject measured twice (before/after, two sites on one person, a "difference" column already given).
Reduce to differences \(d_i\): 1-sample t on the differences, \(T=\dfrac{\bar d-\mu_0}{s_d/\sqrt n}\sim t_{n-1}\). 2024 Q3 (forearm − upper arm, difference SD given = the tell).
Two samples · INDEPENDENT two separate groups. Now check the SDs:
SDs ≈ equal sample SDs close, or told to assume equal variance.
Pooled 2-sample t: \(S_p^2=\dfrac{(m-1)S_X^2+(n-1)S_Y^2}{m+n-2}\), \(T=\dfrac{\bar X-\bar Y}{S_p\sqrt{1/m+1/n}}\sim t_{m+n-2}\) (#64,#74). CI: #65. 2023 Q4 (both SD 0.17 → pooled); 2025 Q3.
SDs clearly unequal one SD much bigger, or told unequal.
Welch 2-sample t: \(T=\dfrac{\bar X-\bar Y}{\sqrt{S_X^2/m+S_Y^2/n}}\), \(t_{\text{df}}\) via Welch/conservative \(\min(m-1,n-1)\) (#66,#75). CI: #66.
③-P Proportions
One proportion \(Z=\dfrac{\hat P-p_0}{\sqrt{p_0(1-p_0)/n}}\sim N(0,1)\) (#73). CI: \(\hat P\pm z\sqrt{\hat P(1-\hat P)/n}\) (#67).
Two proportions pooled \(\hat P=\frac{X+Y}{m+n}\), \(Z=\dfrac{\hat P_X-\hat P_Y}{\sqrt{\hat P(1-\hat P)(1/m+1/n)}}\) (#76). CI: #68.
④ χ² test of association (categorical × categorical)
expected \(=\dfrac{\text{row total}\times\text{col total}}{\text{grand total}}\); \(\chi^2=\sum\dfrac{(O-E)^2}{E}\sim\chi^2_{(r-1)(c-1)}\) (#77). Compare to χ²-table. 2023 Q5(e), 2024 Q5(e), 2025 Q2(d).
⑤ ANOVA (means of 3+ groups)
One factor one grouping variable → one-way. df: Factor \(d-1\), Error \(n-d\), Total \(n-1\). \(F=MSF/MSE\sim F_{d-1,\,n-d}\) (#79–80). 2023 Q5, 2024 Q4.
Two factors two grouping variables (+ interaction) → two-way. Rows: A, B, Interaction, Residuals, Total (#81). 2025 Q4.
⑥ Regression (relationship between two numeric variables)
Model \(Y=\beta_0+\beta_1 x+\varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\) (#83). Read lm(): Estimate = coefficients, Std. Error → CI for slope \(\hat\beta_1\pm t_{n-2}\,SE\), Pr(>|t|) = significance of \(\beta_1\), Residual df + 2 = \(n\). 2023 Q6, 2024 Q5, 2025 Q5.
The paired vs independent fork is the classic mark-loser. Tell-tale signs of paired: the question gives a "difference" column with its own mean and SD, or the two measurements come from the same unit (same person's two arms, before/after). If you'd need 1/m+1/n you're doing it wrong — paired uses a single differenced sample.
Pooled vs Welch: in this course, if the two sample SDs are close (or the question says "assuming equal variances") use pooled; if one SD is much larger, use Welch and state your choice. 2023 Q4 gave identical SDs (0.17, 0.17) — a deliberate nudge to pool.

⏱️ Exam-day strategy

The 10-minute planning playbook

You get 10 minutes of perusal before writing. Do not stare — triage.

  1. Skim all 5 questions and label each with its area (Q1 probability, Q2 estimator/joint, Q3 two-sample inference, Q4 ANOVA, Q5 regression — that's the usual layout).
  2. Score each part's difficulty in the margin: ✓ (instant), ~ (routine), ? (need to think). This becomes your running order.
  3. Spot the method for every "perform a test / construct a CI" part using the decision tree — write the statistic name next to it so you don't re-derive under pressure.
  4. Locate the formulas you'll need on the sheet (note the numbers, e.g. "#65 pooled CI", "#77 χ²") and the right table (t vs χ² vs F).
  5. Start with your strongest full question when writing begins — bank marks and confidence early.

Mark-per-minute budgeting

80 marks in 120 minutes = exactly 1.5 min per mark. There is no slack built into that, so aim for ~1.4 min/mark while working and bank the difference as a checking buffer. Post a marks-to-minutes budget in your head at the start of each question:

If you hit a part's hard stop, write down the method and move on — an examiner gives method marks even without the final number. A stalled 2-mark part is not worth a rushed 7-mark part elsewhere.

"Show your working" conventions

  • State the formula before numbers. Write \(T=\dfrac{\bar X-\mu_0}{S/\sqrt n}\) then substitute — that line alone earns method marks.
  • Define every symbol you introduce. "\(\mu_1\) = true mean protein of 100g sheep pâté" — the exam explicitly asks you to "explain notation."
  • State the distribution & df of your test statistic (\(\sim t_{10}\), \(\sim F_{2,87}\), \(\sim\chi^2_2\)) — required and easy marks.
  • Quote the table value you used (e.g. "\(t_{10,0.975}=2.23\)", "\(\chi^2_{2,0.95}=5.99\)").
  • Every test ends with a decision AND a context sentence. Two separate things — see below.

The 3-significant-figure rule

Instruction on every paper: "Give at least 3 significant figures." This governs reported answers, not intermediate steps. Keep full precision on your calculator through the whole calculation and round only the final number. Rounding \(S_p\) or a test statistic early and carrying it forward is a guaranteed accuracy-mark loss. Where a part says "to two significant figures" (e.g. report the t-statistic), obey that part's instruction for its reported value only.

What a full-marks "conclusion in context" looks like

Almost every inference part ends with "conclusion in context." Full marks = two sentences: the statistical decision, then the plain-English meaning in the story's own words.

Statistical: "Since \(P = 0.003 \lt 0.05\), we reject \(H_0\)."
In context: "There is strong evidence that the mean plaque-score difference is not the same across the three toothpastes." (2023 Q5 style)
From a CI: "We are 95% confident the true mean difference in protein content between 100g sheep and goat pâté lies between 1.28 g and 1.72 g. As the interval is entirely above 0, sheep pâté has significantly more protein on average." (2023 Q4(b), verified.)
Losing the context mark is the most common avoidable slip. "Reject \(H_0\)" alone is half the conclusion — you must translate it back into pâté / blood pressure / plaque / tree-age language, using the units and the direction of the effect.

Surprises worth knowing (from the papers themselves)

  • The "claim" twist. After a standard test, a part often bolts on a claim with a non-zero null — "at least 2g more" (2023 Q4c), "3mmHg larger" (2024 Q3c). You must shift the null to \(\mu_1-\mu_2=2\) (or 3) and go one-sided. Easy to miss.
  • CI for a standard deviation. 2024 Q3(d) asked for a CI for \(\sigma\) — that's the χ² interval (#63), not a t-interval. A different table entirely.
  • Conceptual, no-calculation parts. 2024 Q4(e) "why multiple t-tests is flawed", 2025 Q5(e) "how CI vs prediction-interval width behaves as \(n\to\infty\)". Marks for understanding, not arithmetic.
  • Two-way ANOVA arrived in 2025 (Q4, mouth-rinse × flossing, with an interaction row and interaction plots). Expect it to stay — know how to fill a two-way table and read an interaction plot.
  • Everything is applied. Sheep pâté, blood pressure, pollution, geysers, tree frogs — the context changes but the method set is fixed. Don't be thrown by the story; extract \(n,\bar x,s\) and route it.
