Ch 1 · First-order ODEs 2020 papers only

Write downOne-liner
🧠Separable: \(y'=f(t)g(y)\ \Rightarrow\ \int\frac{dy}{g(y)}=\int f(t)\,dt\)List the equilibria \(g(y)=0\) before dividing, or you silently lose solutions.
🧠Linear: \(y'+p y=q\), \(I=e^{\int p\,dt}\), \((Iy)'=Iq\)Put it in standard form first. Divide by the coefficient of \(y'\) (2020 S2 Q2).
🧠Equilibria: \(y'=f(y)\), \(f(c)=0\); stable if \(f\gt0\) below, \(f\lt0\) aboveDraw a sign chart of \(f(y)\). Arrows pointing in means stable. A \(t\)-factor like \((t-2)\) never creates an equilibrium (2020 S2 Q1).
🧠Euler: \(y_{n+1}=y_n+h\,f(t_n,y_n)\)Tabulate \(n,\ t_n,\ y_n,\ f(t_n,y_n)\). One row per step, exact fractions.
🧠Growth \(P=P_0e^{rt}\); cooling \(T=T_m+(T_0-T_m)e^{-kt}\)Two data points give the two unknowns. Take logs of a ratio to find \(k\).
🧠Mixing: \(\frac{dA}{dt}=(\text{conc in})(\text{rate in})-\frac{A}{V}(\text{rate out})\)Check units: kg/min on both sides.
🧠Substitution \(y=xu\) makes \(y'=F(y/x)\) separable\(y'=u+xu'\). This was the whole of 2020 S1 Q5.

Ch 2 · Second-order linear ODEs 2020 papers only

Write downOne-liner
🧠\(ay''+by'+cy=0\ \to\ a\lambda^2+b\lambda+c=0\)The discriminant \(b^2-4ac\) decides which of the three forms to write.
🧠Distinct: \(c_1e^{\lambda_1t}+c_2e^{\lambda_2t}\) · Repeated: \((c_1+c_2t)e^{\lambda t}\)Leaving out the \(t\) in the repeated case gives only one solution, so full marks are impossible.
🧠Complex \(\alpha\pm\beta i\): \(e^{\alpha t}(c_1\cos\beta t+c_2\sin\beta t)\)\(\beta\) goes inside the trig functions, \(\alpha\) in the exponential. 2020 S1 Q7: \(4\lambda^2-4\lambda+5=0\) gives \(\tfrac12\pm i\).
🧠\(y=y_H+y_P\); apply the ICs to the full \(y\)Applying the ICs to \(y_H\) alone is the most common ODE slip.
🧠Guess \(y_P\): poly → poly · \(e^{kt}\to Ae^{kt}\) · \(\cos/\sin\to A\cos+B\sin\)Always include both \(\cos\) and \(\sin\), even if \(r(t)\) has only one of them.
🧠Overlap rule: guess solves the homogeneous equation → multiply by \(t\) (again if needed)Compare the guess with \(y_H\) before differentiating.
🧠Reduction of order: \(y_2=u\,y_1\)2020 S2 Q3: find \(n\) with \(y=x^n\), then set \(y_2=u\,x^n\).
🧠Spring \(mx''+\beta x'+kx=0\): \(\beta^2-4mk\ \gt,=,\lt\ 0\) → over / critical / underWith no damping, \(\omega=\sqrt{k/m}\) and the motion is \(A\cos(\omega t-\phi)\).

Ch 3 · Surfaces, contours & cross-sections 9 of 11 papers

Write downOne-liner
🧠Contour: set \(f(x,y)=c\), draw in the \(xy\)-plane"Contour through \((2,1)\)": first compute \(c=f(2,1)\) (2024 S2 Q1a, 2025 S1 Q3a).
🧠Complete the square: \(x^2+2px=(x+p)^2-p^2\)It turns every quadratic contour into a standard conic with a visible centre.
🧠Ellipse \(\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}+\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1\) · circle when \(a=b\)Semi-axes \(a\) (horizontal) and \(b\) (vertical), centre \((h,k)\).
🧠Hyperbola \(\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=\pm1\), asymptotes \(y-k=\pm\frac ba(x-h)\)The sign on the right decides the orientation: \(+1\) opens left-right, \(-1\) opens up-down.
🧠Intercepts: set \(y=0\) for \(x\)-intercepts, \(x=0\) for \(y\)-intercepts, exactly"Label all intercepts" is a stated mark. If there are none, write "no \(x\)-intercepts".
🧠Cross-section \(y=k\): plot \(z\) against \(x\), e.g. \(z=\sqrt x+k^2-3\) (2025 S2 Q1b)For "show the cross-sections are parabolas", rearrange to \(x=(\ldots)^2\) or \(z=a(\ldots)^2+\ldots\) form.
🧠Quadrics: paraboloid \(z=x^2+y^2\) · saddle \(z=x^2-y^2\) · cone \(z^2=x^2+y^2\) · ellipsoidFor "match the function to the plot" parts, look at the contours: circles, hyperbolas or lines.
📄General conic \(ax^2+by^2+cx+dy+e=0\) 2023 S2 sheet only\(ab\gt0\) gives an ellipse or circle (or it degenerates), \(ab\lt0\) a hyperbola, and \(a=0\) or \(b=0\) a parabola.

Ch 4 · Limits, partials & approximation limits 9 / 11 · tangent plane 5 / 11

Write downOne-liner
🧠DNE: two paths, two different limitsTry \(y=mx\) first. If you get the same answer for every \(m\), try \(y=kx^2\) or \(x=ky^2\) (2024 S1 Q2, 2023 S2 Q8).
🧠Exists: squeeze \(|f-L|\le g\to0\), or polar \(r\to0\)Agreement along paths never proves a limit exists. You need a bound.
🧠Continuous at \((a,b)\iff\lim f=f(a,b)\)Three checks: \(f(a,b)\) is defined, the limit exists, and they are equal. "Removable discontinuity" means the limit exists but differs from \(f(a,b)\) (2023 S1 Q2).
🧠\(f_x(0,0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(h,0)-f(0,0)}{h}\)Use it for piecewise functions at the origin. The rules don't apply at the join (2025 S2 Q4b, 2025 S1 Q2c).
🧠Clairaut: \(f_{xy}=f_{yx}\)A free check on your second partials before you build \(D\) or \(Q\).
📄\(P(x,y)=f+f_x(x-a)+f_y(y-b)\) = tangent planeWrite the plane as \(z=\dots\). Every coefficient is evaluated at the base point.
📄\(Q=P+\tfrac12f_{xx}(x-a)^2+f_{xy}(x-a)(y-b)+\tfrac12f_{yy}(y-b)^2\)There is no \(\tfrac12\) on the mixed term.
🧠\(\Delta f\approx f_x\Delta x+f_y\Delta y\); worst case \(|f_x|E_1+|f_y|E_2\)"Estimate the change / maximum error" is this line, not \(P\) evaluated at a point.

Ch 5 · Gradient, directional derivatives & chain rule gradient 9 / 11 · chain 8 / 11

Write downOne-liner
🧠\(\nabla f=(f_x,f_y,f_z)\), \(\perp\) to level setsSo the normal to the level curve \(f=c\) at \(P\) is \(\nabla f(P)\).
📄\(D_{\mathbf u}f=\nabla f\cdot\frac{\mathbf u}{\lVert\mathbf u\rVert}\)"Towards \(Q\) from \(P\)": \(\mathbf u=Q-P\). Always normalise.
🧠Max increase: direction \(\nabla f\), rate \(\lVert\nabla f\rVert\) · max decrease: \(-\nabla f\)Asked in 2025 S2 Q3b, 2024 S2 Q4c, 2024 S1 Q1b–c, 2023 S1 Q1b–c and 2023 S2 Q3a.
🧠No change: \(\mathbf u\perp\nabla f\), i.e. \(\pm(f_y,-f_x)\)Give both directions. There are 2 marks for each (2025 S1 Q1c, 2023 S1 Q1d).
🧠Unknown \(\nabla f=(p,q)\): one equation per given directional derivative2025 S1 Q1a and 2022 S1 Q6 run the directional-derivative formula backwards.
📄\(\frac{dz}{dt}=z_x\frac{dx}{dt}+z_y\frac{dy}{dt}\)Draw the tree. There is one product per branch.
🧠Two parameters: \(f_u=f_xx_u+f_yy_u\), \(f_v=f_xx_v+f_yy_v\)The sheet shows only \(d/dt\). Answer "in terms of \(u,v\)" by substituting back.
🧠\(g=f(ax+by)\): \(g_x=af'\), \(g_y=bf'\) \(\Rightarrow\ a\,g_y=b\,g_x\)The "show that" PDE identity: set \(s=ax+by\) and use the chain rule on each (2025 S2 Q3a, 2022 S1 Q4).
📄\(F(x,y)=0\Rightarrow y'=-F_x/F_y\)First find the point(s) on the curve. There may be two (2022 S1 Q5).

Ch 6 · Optimisation critical pts 10 / 11 · Lagrange 11 / 11

Write downOne-liner
🧠Critical point: \(\nabla f=\mathbf 0\) (or \(\nabla f\) undefined)Solve by cases, e.g. "\(e^{-2x}\neq0\), so …" (2025 S2 Q6). Never divide by a variable that could be zero.
📄\(D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2\) at the pointEvaluate at each critical point separately.
🧠\(D\gt0,f_{xx}\gt0\): min · \(D\gt0,f_{xx}\lt0\): max · \(D\lt0\): saddle · \(D=0\): ?The table itself is not on the sheet. \(D=0\) is inconclusive, not a saddle.
🧠Global on closed region: interior crit pts + each edge + cornersParametrise each edge, e.g. \(x=0,\ 0\le y\le1\), and do 1-D calculus. Compare every value (2025 S1 Q5, 2024 S1 Q5).
🧠"Does \(f\) attain a global max?": find a path where \(f\to\pm\infty\)E.g. \(f(x,0)\to+\infty\) as \(x\to-\infty\) (2023 S1 Q5c, 2023 S2 Q1b).
📄\(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\)The sheet stops here …
🧠… plus \(g=\text{const}\): 3 (or 4) equations, 3 (or 4) unknowns… and the constraint equation is the line students forget.
🧠Closed & bounded constraint \(\Rightarrow\) max & min exist (EVT)Circles, ellipses and spheres are closed and bounded. Lines and planes are not.
🧠Distance problems: minimise \(D^2\), not \(D\)Same minimiser, and no square roots to differentiate (2021 S1 Q7).

Ch 7 · Curves & vector fields work 11 / 11 · length 9 / 11

Write downOne-liner
🧠Segment \(A\to B\): \(\mathbf r(t)=A+t(B-A)\), \(0\le t\le1\)Asked outright in 2025 S1 Q8e(i).
🧠Circle \((h+r\cos t,\,k+r\sin t)\) · ellipse \((h+a\cos t,\,k+b\sin t)\)Increasing \(t\) runs anticlockwise. "Indicate the starting point and direction" is worth marks.
🧠\(\mathbf v=\mathbf r'\), \(\mathbf a=\mathbf r''\), speed \(=\lVert\mathbf r'\rVert\)Speed is a scalar. Velocity is a vector.
🧠\(\mathbf v=\int\mathbf a\,dt+\mathbf v_0\), \(\mathbf r=\int\mathbf v\,dt+\mathbf r_0\)Projectiles: the constants come from the initial conditions, component by component (2025 S2 Q7, 2025 S1 Q7a).
📄\(L=\int_a^b\lVert\mathbf r'(t)\rVert\,dt\)Expect \(\sin^2+\cos^2\) or a perfect square under the root.
📄\(W=\int_a^b\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\)Par it → Diff it → \(\mathbf F\) in \(t\) → Dot it → Int it.
🧠Conservative on \(\mathbb R^2\) \(\iff P_y=Q_x\) (3-D: all three cross-partials match)With unknown \(A,B\), match coefficients of \(P_y\) and \(Q_x\) (2024 S2 Q8a).
🧠Potential: \(f=\int P\,dx+h(y)\), then \(f_y=Q\) fixes \(h\)Check by computing \(\nabla f\). Constants of integration can be dropped.
🧠\(W=f(\mathbf r(b))-f(\mathbf r(a))\); closed loop \(\Rightarrow W=0\)Ugly path plus conservative field means use the potential (2025 S2 Q8c, 2024 S1/S2 Q8).

✍️ Exam-day method rituals

"You must justify all answers." These are the write-ups that collect every method mark. Learn the shape of each one and fill in the numbers on the day. Each worked instance uses fresh numbers (not from any past paper) and is checked with SymPy.

1 · Critical-point classification: the 5-line write-up

  1. Partials: \(f_x=\ldots,\quad f_y=\ldots\)
  2. Solve \(f_x=f_y=0\) by cases, and list the critical points.
  3. Second partials: \(f_{xx}, f_{xy}, f_{yy}\), so \(D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2\) as a formula.
  4. Table: one row per point, showing \(D\), the sign of \(f_{xx}\), and the verdict.
  5. Conclusion sentence naming each point, its type, and \(f\) there if asked.
Worked instance: \(f(x,y)=2x^3+6xy+3y^2\)
  1. \(f_x=6x^2+6y,\quad f_y=6x+6y\).
  2. \(f_y=0\Rightarrow y=-x\). Substituting, \(6x^2-6x=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=1\). The critical points are \((0,0)\) and \((1,-1)\).
  3. \(f_{xx}=12x,\ f_{xy}=6,\ f_{yy}=6\), so \(D=72x-36\).
  4. Point\(D\)\(f_{xx}\)Type
    \((0,0)\)\(-36\lt0\)0saddle
    \((1,-1)\)\(36\gt0\)\(12\gt0\)local min
  5. Therefore \(f\) has a saddle point at \((0,0)\) and a local minimum at \((1,-1)\), where \(f(1,-1)=-1\).
If a question says "classify one of these" (2021 S1 Q8b, 2022 S1 Q7b), still do the table row properly. If \(D=0\), write "the second-derivative test is inconclusive", then compare \(f\) along a line through the point.

2 · Lagrange multipliers: the 4-line write-up

  1. Set up: "Maximise/minimise \(f=\ldots\) subject to \(g=\ldots=c\). By Lagrange, \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\)."
  2. The system, written out in full, including the constraint: \(f_x=\lambda g_x\), \(f_y=\lambda g_y\), (\(f_z=\lambda g_z\)), \(g=c\).
  3. Solve by cases, e.g. "if \(x=0\) then …; otherwise divide …", and list all candidate points.
  4. Evaluate \(f\) at every candidate and justify: "The constraint set is closed and bounded, so by the EVT the max is … at … and the min is … at …"
Worked instance: extremes of \(f=x^2+y^2\) on the hyperbola \(xy=4\)
  1. Minimise/maximise \(f=x^2+y^2\) subject to \(g=xy=4\). By Lagrange, \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\).
  2. \(2x=\lambda y,\quad 2y=\lambda x,\quad xy=4\).
  3. \(xy=4\) means \(x,y\neq0\). Multiplying the first equation by \(x\) and the second by \(y\): \(2x^2=\lambda xy=2y^2\), so \(y=\pm x\). \(y=-x\) would give \(-x^2=4\), which is impossible. So \(y=x\), \(x^2=4\), and the candidates are \((2,2)\) and \((-2,-2)\), with \(\lambda=2\).
  4. \(f(2,2)=f(-2,-2)=8\). The hyperbola is unbounded (\(f\to\infty\) along it), so there is no maximum. The minimum is 8 at \((\pm2,\pm2)\) (same signs). It exists because \(f\to\infty\) at both ends of each branch.
Line 4 is where the marks go. Evaluate \(f\) at every candidate, and say why a max or min exists: closed and bounded (EVT), or a growth argument when the constraint is unbounded.

3 · Conservative field → potential → work: the 3-line write-up

  1. Test: "\(\mathbf F=(P,Q)\) with \(P_y=\ldots=Q_x\) on all of \(\mathbb R^2\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative."
  2. Potential: "\(f=\int P\,dx=\ldots+h(y)\). Then \(f_y=\ldots=Q\) gives \(h'(y)=\ldots\), so \(f=\ldots\)." Check that \(\nabla f=\mathbf F\).
  3. Work: "By the fundamental theorem for line integrals, \(W=f(\mathbf r(b))-f(\mathbf r(a))=\ldots\)."
Worked instance: \(\mathbf F=(2xy+3,\ x^2-4y)\), from \((0,0)\) to \((1,2)\) along any path
  1. \(P_y=2x=Q_x\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.
  2. \(f=\int(2xy+3)\,dx=x^2y+3x+h(y)\). Then \(f_y=x^2+h'(y)=x^2-4y\), so \(h=-2y^2\), giving \(f=x^2y+3x-2y^2\).
  3. \(W=f(1,2)-f(0,0)=(2+3-8)-0=-3\).
If \(P_y\neq Q_x\), write "\(P_y\neq Q_x\), so \(\mathbf F\) is not conservative, and the work depends on the path." Then use the sheet's direct formula. 2021 S2 Q8 tests exactly this switch.

4 · Continuity at the origin: the argument templates

Template A (NOT continuous, or the limit does not exist):
  1. "Along the path \(y=0\): \(f(x,0)=\ldots\to L_1\)."
  2. "Along the path \(y=\ldots\): \(f=\ldots\to L_2\)."
  3. "Since \(L_1\neq L_2\), \(\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}f\) does not exist, so \(f\) is not continuous at \((0,0)\)."
Template B (continuous):
  1. "For \((x,y)\neq(0,0)\): \(|f(x,y)-f(0,0)|\le\ldots\)", a bound built from \(|x|\le\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\) and \(\frac{x^2}{x^2+y^2}\le1\).
  2. "The bound \(\to0\) as \((x,y)\to(0,0)\), so by the squeeze theorem \(\lim f=f(0,0)\)."
  3. "Hence \(f\) is continuous at \((0,0)\)."
Worked instances: \(g=\dfrac{x^2-y^2}{x^2+y^2}\) (A) and \(h=\dfrac{x^2y}{x^2+y^2}\), \(h(0,0)=0\) (B)

A. Along \(y=0\): \(g=1\to1\). Along \(x=0\): \(g=-1\to-1\). Since \(1\neq-1\), the limit does not exist, so \(g\) cannot be made continuous at \((0,0)\) by any choice of \(g(0,0)\).

B. \(|h(x,y)-0|=\dfrac{x^2}{x^2+y^2}\,|y|\le|y|\to0\). By the squeeze theorem \(\lim h=0=h(0,0)\), so \(h\) is continuous at \((0,0)\).

Same limit along several paths proves nothing. Only a bound (Template B) proves the limit exists. For "find \(c\) so \(f\) is continuous" (2021 S2 Q3), use Template B to find the limit, then set \(c\) equal to it.

5 · Two quick rituals worth a line each

Directional derivative (3 lines): \(\nabla f(P)=\ldots\), then \(\hat{\mathbf u}=\mathbf u/\lVert\mathbf u\rVert=\ldots\), then \(D_{\mathbf u}f=\nabla f\cdot\hat{\mathbf u}=\ldots\) "per unit distance".
Contour sketch (4 lines): \(c=f(\text{point})\), then complete the square into standard form, then name the curve with its centre and axes (or asymptotes), then list the intercepts and draw with labels.
