Your Exam Weapon: the Formula Sheet, Decoded

The one-page formula sheet printed at the back of every MATH1052 paper, taken apart item by item.

The sheet is tiny: eight formulas and no definitions, no ODE methods, no integrals and no classification rules. You also have no calculator. So the sheet only helps you if you already know when each formula applies, what every symbol means, and everything the sheet leaves out. This page covers all three.

How to use a tiny sheet in a no-calculator exam

The paper is 120 minutes plus 10 minutes planning, 100 marks, 8 questions, with no calculator and no notes. "You must justify all answers." The sheet is the last page, and you may detach it. Anything you write on the sheet is not marked. These are its eight items, in printed order, with the chapter each belongs to:

The sheet gives you formulas, never methods. It does not say that critical points need \(\nabla f=\mathbf 0\), what the sign of \(D\) means, that \(\mathbf u\) must be a unit vector (it only hints with \(\mathbf u/\lVert\mathbf u\rVert\)), that a Lagrange system also needs the constraint \(g=0\), or how to test whether a field is conservative. Those decisions carry most of the marks. The "NOT on the sheet" checklist is where the real marks are.

The four weapon rules

  1. Name the item in your answer. Write "By the directional-derivative formula, \(D_{\mathbf u}f=\nabla f\cdot\mathbf u/\lVert\mathbf u\rVert\) …" as the first line. Quoting the formula earns the method mark even if the arithmetic slips later.
  2. Copy the formula, then substitute symbol by symbol. Write \(f(a,b)\), \(f_x(a,b)\), \(f_y(a,b)\) as a small table first, then plug in. With no calculator, most errors are copying errors.
  3. Plan with the sheet open. In the 10 planning minutes, tag each question with the sheet item(s) it needs. A question that matches none of the eight items is testing something you must know cold (limits, contours, ODEs, potentials).
  4. Exact answers only. Leave \(\sqrt{73}\), \(\ln 2\), \(10\pi\) and \(\tfrac{36}{5}\) as they are. Give a decimal only when the question hands you one, as 2025 S2 Q2 did with "\(\ln 2\approx0.6931\)".

How the sheet has changed across years

Taken from the last page of every question paper we have. The content of the eight items has been stable since 2022. What changes between years is the notation, and that can throw you if you only drill one year's paper.

Item 1 · Linear & quadratic approximations Ch4

As printed

$$P(x,y)=f(a,b)+f_x(a,b)(x-a)+f_y(a,b)(y-b)$$ $$Q(x,y)=P(x,y)+\tfrac12 f_{xx}(a,b)(x-a)^2+f_{xy}(a,b)(x-a)(y-b)+\tfrac12 f_{yy}(a,b)(y-b)^2$$

(The sheet writes the first three terms of \(Q\) out in full. Here they are shortened to \(P\).)

Every symbol

  • \((a,b)\) is the base point. It is always a point where \(f\) and its derivatives come out as clean exact numbers. \((x,y)\) is the nearby point you want to estimate at.
  • \(f_x(a,b)\) and \(f_y(a,b)\) are the first partials evaluated at the base point. They are numbers, not functions.
  • \(f_{xx},f_{xy},f_{yy}\) are the second partials at \((a,b)\). By Clairaut, \(f_{xy}=f_{yx}\) for the smooth functions in this course.
  • \(P\) is the tangent plane. The surface \(z=P(x,y)\) is the tangent plane to \(z=f(x,y)\) at \((a,b,f(a,b))\).
"tangent plane", "linear approximation / linearisation", "estimate \(f(1.02,\,2.97)\)", "use the quadratic approximation", "approximate change in …".
Micro example. \(f(x,y)=x e^{y}\) at \((a,b)=(2,0)\). Estimate \(f(2.2,\,0.1)\).
Values at \((2,0)\): \(f=2\), \(f_x=e^y=1\), \(f_y=xe^y=2\); \(f_{xx}=0\), \(f_{xy}=e^y=1\), \(f_{yy}=xe^y=2\).
\(P(x,y)=2+(x-2)+2y\), so \(P(2.2,0.1)=2+0.2+0.2=\) 2.4.
\(Q(x,y)=P+\tfrac12(0)(x-2)^2+(1)(x-2)y+\tfrac12(2)y^2\), so \(Q(2.2,0.1)=2.4+0.02+0.01=\) 2.43.
(The true value is \(2.2e^{0.1}\approx2.4314\). The quadratic term fixed most of the gap.)
The classic misuse. Halving the mixed term. The \(\tfrac12\) goes only on \(f_{xx}\) and \(f_{yy}\). The mixed term is \(f_{xy}(x-a)(y-b)\), with no half, because \(f_{xy}\) and \(f_{yx}\) each contribute half. A close second: substituting the estimate point into \(f_x\) instead of the base point. Every coefficient is evaluated at \((a,b)\).
For a "tangent plane" question, write the final answer as \(z=\dots\), for example \(z=2+(x-2)+2y\), i.e. \(z=x+2y\). "Estimate \(f(\dots)\)" questions want a number. 2025 S2 Q2 wanted both: the plane in (b) (4 marks), then the estimate in (c) (4 marks).

Item 2 · Directional derivative Ch5

As printed (2025 S2 form)

$$D_{\mathbf u}f(a,b)=\nabla f(a,b)\cdot\frac{\mathbf u}{\lVert\mathbf u\rVert}$$

Every symbol

  • \(\nabla f(a,b)=\big(f_x(a,b),\,f_y(a,b)\big)\) is the gradient, or \((f_x,f_y,f_z)\) in 3-D. Its definition is not on the sheet.
  • \(\mathbf u\) is the direction you are given, in any length. \(\mathbf u/\lVert\mathbf u\rVert\) is the unit vector in that direction. The sheet makes you normalise.
  • The output is a scalar: the rate of change of \(f\) per unit distance travelled in direction \(\mathbf u\).
"rate of change of \(T\) at \(P\) in the direction of \(\mathbf v\)", "… towards the point \(Q\)" (then \(\mathbf u=Q-P\)), "slope of the surface in direction …".
Micro example. \(f(x,y)=x^2+3xy\) at \((1,2)\) in the direction of \(\mathbf u=(3,4)\).
\(\nabla f=(2x+3y,\ 3x)\), so \(\nabla f(1,2)=(8,3)\). \(\lVert\mathbf u\rVert=5\).
\(D_{\mathbf u}f(1,2)=(8,3)\cdot\tfrac{(3,4)}{5}=\tfrac{24+12}{5}=\) \(\tfrac{36}{5}\).
Bonus (not on the sheet): the maximum rate of change at \((1,2)\) is \(\lVert\nabla f\rVert=\sqrt{73}\), in direction \((8,3)\).
The classic misuse. Forgetting to divide by \(\lVert\mathbf u\rVert\), which here would give 36 instead of \(36/5\). A second misuse is "towards the origin from \(P\)" read as the direction \(P\). It is \(\mathbf 0-P=-P\). A third is reversing the problem: in 2025 S1 Q1 and 2022 S1 Q6 you are given directional derivatives and must solve for \(\nabla f\). Set \(\nabla f=(p,q)\), write one equation per given direction, and solve the linear system.
The three follow-up facts not on the sheet, examined almost every year: the direction of greatest increase is \(\nabla f\), the greatest rate is \(\lVert\nabla f\rVert\), and the directions of no change are \(\perp\nabla f\) (in 2-D, \((f_y,-f_x)\) and \((-f_y,f_x)\)). Greatest decrease is \(-\nabla f\) (2021 S1 Q5b).

Item 3 · Chain rule Ch5

As printed

$$\frac{dz}{dt}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dt}+\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dt}\qquad\Big(\text{2025 S2: }\ \frac{df}{dt}=f_x\frac{dx}{dt}+f_y\frac{dy}{dt}\Big)$$

Every symbol

  • \(z=f(x,y)\), where both \(x=x(t)\) and \(y=y(t)\) depend on one parameter \(t\).
  • \(\partial z/\partial x\) and \(\partial z/\partial y\) are computed in \(x,y\), then evaluated at \((x(t),y(t))\).
  • One term per path in the tree diagram \(z\to x\to t\) and \(z\to y\to t\). Multiply along each branch, then add the branches.
"at what rate is … changing", "with the aid of a tree diagram" (2025 S1 Q4), "\(x=u^2,\ y=uv^2\), find \(\partial f/\partial u\)" (2024 S2 Q6), "show that \(a\,g_y=b\,g_x\)" (2025 S2 Q3a), related rates (2024 S1 Q4).
Micro example. \(z=x^2y\), \(x=\cos t\), \(y=\sin t\). Find \(\dfrac{dz}{dt}\) at \(t=\pi/4\).
\(z_x=2xy,\ z_y=x^2\); \(x'=-\sin t,\ y'=\cos t\). At \(t=\pi/4\): \(x=y=\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\), so \(z_x=1,\ z_y=\tfrac12\).
\(\dfrac{dz}{dt}=1\cdot\big(-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\big)+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}=\) \(-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}\).
Check by direct substitution: \(z=\cos^2t\sin t\), so \(z'=-2\cos t\sin^2t+\cos^3t=-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}\). ✓
The classic misuse. The sheet only shows the one-parameter version. With two parameters (\(x=x(u,v),\ y=y(u,v)\)) every \(d/dt\) becomes \(\partial/\partial u\): \(\ f_u=f_x\,x_u+f_y\,y_u\). A second misuse is leaving the answer in mixed variables when asked "in terms of \(u\) and \(v\)". Substitute \(x=x(u,v)\) and \(y=y(u,v)\) back into \(f_x,f_y\).
If the question says "check by expressing \(w\) directly" (2025 S1 Q4b), substitute first and differentiate. That is a free confirmation of your tree answer, and the marks are for doing it both ways.

Item 4 · Implicit differentiation Ch5

As printed

$$F(x,y)=0\ \Longrightarrow\ y'(a)=-\frac{F_x(a,b)}{F_y(a,b)}$$

Every symbol

  • \(F(x,y)=0\) is the curve, with everything moved to one side.
  • \((a,b)\) is a point on the curve, so \(F(a,b)=0\). \(y'(a)\) is the slope \(dy/dx\) of the curve there.
  • You need \(F_y(a,b)\neq0\). If \(F_y(a,b)=0\), the tangent is vertical (or the formula fails).
"tangent line to the curve \(y\ln x+xe^y-e^2=0\) at \(x=1\)" (2024 S2 Q3), "tangent line(s) at \(x=0\)" (2022 S1 Q5), any curve you can't solve for \(y\).
Micro example. Find the tangent line to \(x^2+xy+y^3=3\) at \((1,1)\).
\(F=x^2+xy+y^3-3\). Check: \(F(1,1)=1+1+1-3=0\) ✓. \(F_x=2x+y=3\), \(F_y=x+3y^2=4\).
\(y'(1)=-\tfrac34\), so the tangent line is \(y=1-\tfrac34(x-1)\), i.e. \(y=\tfrac74-\tfrac34x\).
The classic misuse. Being given only \(x=a\) and not solving for \(b\) first. In 2022 S1 Q5, \(x=0\) gives \(y\cos0=y^2\), so \(y=0\) or \(y=1\): two points, two tangent lines. Also watch for the flipped fraction \(-F_y/F_x\), and a dropped minus sign.
When a tangent line needs a value like \(y=2\) at \(x=1\), state it ("at \(x=1\): \(y\ln1+e^y=e^2\), so \(y=2\)"). Finding the point is its own mark.

Item 5 · Determinant of the Hessian Ch6

As printed

$$D=\begin{vmatrix}f_{xx}(a,b)&f_{xy}(a,b)\\ f_{yx}(a,b)&f_{yy}(a,b)\end{vmatrix}=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2$$

Every symbol

  • \((a,b)\) is a critical point: \(\nabla f(a,b)=\mathbf 0\). The sheet doesn't say this.
  • Vertical bars mean determinant: \(ad-bc\). With Clairaut, \(f_{xy}=f_{yx}\), so \(D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2\).
  • What the sign of \(D\) means is not on the sheet. Memorise the classification table: \(D\gt0\) with \(f_{xx}\gt0\) is a local min, \(D\gt0\) with \(f_{xx}\lt0\) is a local max, \(D\lt0\) is a saddle, and \(D=0\) is inconclusive.
"find and classify all critical points" (2025 S2 Q6, 2023 S2 Q1, 2023 S1 Q5, 2021 S2 Q5 …), "determine the nature of the stationary point".
Micro example. \(f(x,y)=x^3-3x+y^2\).
\(f_x=3x^2-3=0\) gives \(x=\pm1\), and \(f_y=2y=0\) gives \(y=0\). Critical points: \((1,0)\) and \((-1,0)\).
\(f_{xx}=6x,\ f_{xy}=0,\ f_{yy}=2\), so \(D=12x\).
\((1,0)\): \(D=12\gt0\) and \(f_{xx}=6\gt0\), so local min. \((-1,0)\): \(D=-12\lt0\), so saddle point.
The classic misuse. Computing \(D\) as a function and never plugging in the point, or classifying with \(f_{yy}\) when you meant \(f_{xx}\) (it also works when \(D\gt0\), but say which you used). Calling \(D=0\) a saddle is wrong: it is inconclusive, and you must argue directly from \(f\) near the point. You also lose marks for missing critical points. Solve \(f_x=0,\ f_y=0\) by cases ("either \(y=0\) or …"). Never divide by something that could be zero.

Item 6 · Lagrange multipliers Ch6 · every paper

As printed

$$\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g$$

Every symbol

  • \(f\) is the objective, the thing to maximise or minimise. \(g\) is the constraint, written as \(g(x,y,z)=\text{const}\), or \(g=0\).
  • \(\lambda\) is the multiplier, an unknown scalar. You rarely need its value, but you need it to link the equations.
  • The vector equation gives 2 or 3 scalar equations. The sheet leaves out the constraint equation itself. You must add \(g=\text{const}\) to get 3 or 4 equations in 3 or 4 unknowns.
"Use the method of Lagrange multipliers" (said outright in 2025 S2 Q5, 2025 S1 Q6, 2024 S2 Q5, 2024 S1 Q6, 2023 S2 Q2, 2023 S1 Q6), or hidden: "on the circle / sphere / plane", "Epsilon walks on …", "where should you park to minimise the distance" (2021 S1 Q7).
Micro example. Extremes of \(f=x+2y\) on the circle \(x^2+y^2=5\).
\(\nabla f=(1,2)\), \(\nabla g=(2x,2y)\). The system: \(1=2\lambda x\), \(2=2\lambda y\), \(x^2+y^2=5\).
\(\lambda\neq0\) (otherwise \(1=0\)), so dividing gives \(y=2x\). Then \(5x^2=5\), so \(x=\pm1\): candidates \((1,2)\) and \((-1,-2)\).
\(f(1,2)=5\), \(f(-1,-2)=-5\). The circle is closed and bounded, so the extremes exist: max 5 at (1,2), min −5 at (−1,−2).
The classic misuse. Solving \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\) alone and forgetting to impose the constraint. Close behind: dividing by \(x\) (or \(\lambda\)) without handling the case where it is 0. In \(f=xyz^2\) problems (2025 S2 Q5), \(x=0\) or \(z=0\) are genuine branches, and you must show they give \(f=0\), not the maximum. Also, "highest and lowest" means evaluate \(f\) at every candidate and name both.
End with one sentence of justification: "Since the constraint set is closed and bounded, \(f\) attains a max and a min on it (Extreme Value Theorem), so they are among these candidates." On an unbounded constraint (a line or plane) that sentence is false, so say what you assume instead. In 2025 S2 Q5 (\(xyz^2\) on \(x+y+z=20\)), \(f\) is unbounded on the whole plane: take \(x=y=t\), \(z=20-2t\), \(t\to\infty\). The Lagrange candidate with \(xyz\neq0\) is \((5,5,10)\), where \(f=2500\). Present it as the maximum for positive \(x,y,z\), and say so.

Item 7 · Work along a parametrised curve Ch7

As printed (2025 S2 form)

$$W=\int_a^b\mathbf F\big(\mathbf r(t)\big)\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt$$

Every symbol

  • \(\mathbf r(t)=(x(t),y(t))\) for \(a\le t\le b\) is the path, including its direction: it starts at \(\mathbf r(a)\).
  • \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\) is the force field with \(x=x(t),\ y=y(t)\) substituted. Older sheets write this as "\(\mathbf F(t)\)", which means the same thing.
  • \(\mathbf r'(t)\) is the velocity vector. The dot product \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'\) is a scalar function of \(t\), and you integrate it from \(a\) to \(b\).
"work done by \(\mathbf F\) on a particle moving along \(C\)", "evaluate \(\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\)", or "use the formula \(W=\int\mathbf F(t)\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\)" (2025 S1 Q8e, 2023 S1 Q8b), which forces the direct method.
Micro example. \(\mathbf F(x,y)=(y,\ x^2)\) along \(\mathbf r(t)=(t,t^2)\), \(0\le t\le1\).
Par it: \(x=t,\ y=t^2\). Diff it: \(\mathbf r'=(1,2t)\). \(\mathbf F\) in \(t\): \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))=(t^2,t^2)\).
Dot it: \(t^2+2t^3\). Int it: \(\int_0^1(t^2+2t^3)\,dt=\tfrac13+\tfrac12=\) \(\tfrac56\).
The classic misuse. Grinding through this integral when the field is conservative and the path is ugly, like \(x=t\cos(\pi t),\ y=2t^2\sin(\pi t)\) in 2025 S2 Q8c or \(\mathbf r=(t\cos t,t\sin t)\) in 2024 S1/S2 Q8. There the intended route is \(W=f(\mathbf r(b))-f(\mathbf r(a))\) with a potential \(f\). That route is not on the sheet. The reverse mistake also costs marks: using the potential when the question says "use the formula \(W=\int\ldots\)" (2025 S1 Q8e). Also, writing \(\mathbf F(x,y)\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\) without substituting leaves you integrating \(x\)'s with respect to \(t\).
For a straight segment from \(A\) to \(B\), use \(\mathbf r(t)=A+t(B-A)\), \(0\le t\le1\). This parametrisation is not on the sheet and was asked outright in 2025 S1 Q8e(i).

Item 8 · Arc length Ch7

As printed

$$L=\int_a^b\lVert\mathbf r'(t)\rVert\,dt$$

Every symbol

  • \(\mathbf r'(t)\) is the velocity vector. \(\lVert\mathbf r'(t)\rVert=\sqrt{x'(t)^2+y'(t)^2+z'(t)^2}\) is the speed, which is always \(\ge0\).
  • \([a,b]\) is the time interval. Keep the interval exactly as given.
"length of the curve/path" (2024 S2 Q7b, 2023 S2 Q5, 2023 S1 Q8a), "distance travelled" (2024 S1 Q7b), "find \(a\) so the helix has length 20" (2021 S2 Q4, where you solve \(L(a)=20\)).
Micro example. The helix \(\mathbf r(t)=(3\cos t,\ 3\sin t,\ 4t)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\).
\(\mathbf r'=(-3\sin t,\ 3\cos t,\ 4)\), so \(\lVert\mathbf r'\rVert=\sqrt{9\sin^2t+9\cos^2t+16}=\sqrt{25}=5\).
\(L=\int_0^{2\pi}5\,dt=\) \(10\pi\).
A second one: \(\mathbf r(t)=(e^t\cos t,\ e^t\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le1\). Then \(\lVert\mathbf r'\rVert^2=e^{2t}\big[(\cos t-\sin t)^2+(\sin t+\cos t)^2\big]=2e^{2t}\), so \(L=\sqrt2\int_0^1e^t\,dt=\) \(\sqrt2(e-1)\).
The classic misuse. Squaring and adding the components but forgetting the square root, or integrating \(\lVert\mathbf r(t)\rVert\) (position) instead of \(\lVert\mathbf r'(t)\rVert\) (velocity). No-calculator papers are designed so the thing under the root simplifies with \(\sin^2+\cos^2=1\) or becomes a perfect square. If it doesn't, recheck your derivatives before integrating.
"Speed at \(t=\pi\)" (2024 S1 Q7a) is just \(\lVert\mathbf r'(\pi)\rVert\), with no integral. "Distance travelled" is the integral. Read the question for which one it wants.

What is NOT on the sheet: know these cold

Tick each line only when you can write it from memory in under 20 seconds. Every line below has been needed in at least one past paper, and the sheet gives you none of them.

🌀 The whole ODE toolkit (Ch1–2)

ODE questions appear in the 2020 papers (2020 S1 Q5, Q7, Q10; 2020 S2 Q1–3) but in none of the 2021–2025 papers we have. They are still in the syllabus, and nothing about them is on the sheet.

☐ToolThe one-line version
☐Direct integration\(y'=f(t)\ \Rightarrow\ y=\int f\,dt+C\). Use the IC to find \(C\).
☐Separable\(y'=f(t)g(y)\): first list the equilibria \(g(y)=0\), then \(\int\frac{dy}{g(y)}=\int f\,dt\).
☐Linear first-order\(y'+p(t)y=q(t)\): \(I=e^{\int p\,dt}\), then \((Iy)'=Iq\), so \(y=\frac1I\big(\int Iq\,dt+C\big)\).
☐Equilibria & stability\(y'=f(y)\): equilibria where \(f(y)=0\). A stable equilibrium has \(f\gt0\) below it and \(f\lt0\) above it. Use a sign chart.
☐Euler's method\(y_{n+1}=y_n+h\,f(t_n,y_n)\), \(t_{n+1}=t_n+h\).
☐Characteristic equation\(ay''+by'+cy=0\ \to\ a\lambda^2+b\lambda+c=0\).
☐3 root casesReal distinct: \(c_1e^{\lambda_1t}+c_2e^{\lambda_2t}\). Repeated: \((c_1+c_2t)e^{\lambda t}\). Complex \(\alpha\pm\beta i\): \(e^{\alpha t}(c_1\cos\beta t+c_2\sin\beta t)\).
☐Undetermined coefficientsPolynomial of degree \(n\): try a degree-\(n\) polynomial. \(e^{kt}\): try \(Ae^{kt}\). \(\cos/\sin\,\omega t\): try \(A\cos\omega t+B\sin\omega t\). If the guess overlaps \(y_H\), multiply by \(t\).
☐General solution\(y=y_H+y_P\). Apply the ICs to the full \(y\), never to \(y_H\) alone.
☐Springs\(mx''+\beta x'+kx=0\), \(\Delta=\beta^2-4mk\): \(\Delta\gt0\) over-damped, \(\Delta=0\) critical, \(\Delta\lt0\) under-damped. With no damping, \(\omega=\sqrt{k/m}\).
☐ModelsGrowth/decay: \(P'=rP\), so \(P=P_0e^{rt}\). Newton cooling: \(T'=-k(T-T_m)\), so \(T=T_m+(T_0-T_m)e^{-kt}\). Mixing: rate in − rate out.

∫ Integrals, trig & exponentials (every question uses these)

☐FactOne line
☐Standard integrals\(\int t^n=\frac{t^{n+1}}{n+1}\), \(\int\frac1t=\ln|t|\), \(\int e^{kt}=\frac1ke^{kt}\), \(\int\cos kt=\frac1k\sin kt\), \(\int\sin kt=-\frac1k\cos kt\), \(\int\sec^2t=\tan t\).
☐Substitution / by parts\(\int f(g)g'=F(g)\). \(\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du\): pick \(u\) as the polynomial.
☐Speed-integral tricks\(\sin^2+\cos^2=1\). \(\int\sqrt{1+t^2}\cdot2t\,dt=\tfrac23(1+t^2)^{3/2}\). Aim for a perfect square under the root.
☐Trig values\(\sin,\cos\) at \(0,\frac\pi6,\frac\pi4,\frac\pi3,\frac\pi2,\pi\), as exact surds.
☐Identities\(\cos2\theta=1-2\sin^2\theta=2\cos^2\theta-1\), \(\sin2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta\), \(\sin(a\pm b)\), \(\cos(a\pm b)\). Papers sometimes print one as a hint (2023 S1 Q7, 2025 S1 Q7). Don't rely on that.
☐Exp / log\(e^{a+b}=e^ae^b\), \(\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b\), \(\ln1=0\), \(\frac{d}{dx}\ln u=\frac{u'}{u}\).
☐Limits you need\(\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\sin u}{u}=1\), \(\lim_{u\to0}\frac{e^u-1}{u}=1\). These do the work in 2023 S1 Q2 and 2024 S2 Q2b.

∇ Multivariable definitions & tests

☐FactOne line
☐Gradient\(\nabla f=(f_x,f_y)\) or \((f_x,f_y,f_z)\). It is \(\perp\) to level curves and surfaces, and points uphill.
☐Steepest ascent / descentDirection \(\pm\nabla f\), max rate \(\lVert\nabla f\rVert\). Zero change: \(\mathbf u\perp\nabla f\).
☐Partial derivative from the definition\(f_x(a,b)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(a+h,b)-f(a,b)}{h}\). Needed for piecewise functions at the origin (2025 S2 Q4b, 2025 S1 Q2c).
☐Limit does not existTwo paths with different limits, e.g. \(y=mx\) and \(y=kx^2\) (2024 S1 Q2).
☐Limit existsSqueeze: \(|f-L|\le g\to0\). Or polar: \(x=r\cos\theta\), \(y=r\sin\theta\), with a bound independent of \(\theta\).
☐Continuity at \((a,b)\)\(\lim_{(x,y)\to(a,b)}f=f(a,b)\): the limit exists and equals the value.
☐Error / small-change estimate\(\Delta f\approx f_x\Delta x+f_y\Delta y\). Worst case: \(|\Delta f|\le|f_x|E_1+|f_y|E_2\).
☐Classification tableAt \(\nabla f=\mathbf 0\): \(D\gt0,f_{xx}\gt0\) is a local min. \(D\gt0,f_{xx}\lt0\) is a local max. \(D\lt0\) is a saddle. \(D=0\) is inconclusive.
☐Global extrema on a closed regionInterior critical points, plus each boundary piece (parametrise it and do 1-D calculus), plus the corners. Compare all the values.
☐Conservative-field test\(\mathbf F=(P,Q)\) on \(\mathbb R^2\): conservative \(\iff P_y=Q_x\). In 3-D: \(P_y=Q_x,\ P_z=R_x,\ Q_z=R_y\).
☐Potential construction\(f=\int P\,dx+h(y)\). Differentiate in \(y\), set equal to \(Q\), and solve for \(h'(y)\).
☐Fundamental theorem for line integrals\(\int_C\nabla f\cdot d\mathbf r=f(\text{end})-f(\text{start})\). Path independent, and 0 around any closed loop.

🗺️ Curves, surfaces, motion (Ch3, Ch7)

☐FactOne line
☐Completing the square\(x^2+2px=(x+p)^2-p^2\). This is the first move on every contour sketch.
☐Conic standard formsCircle \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\). Ellipse \(\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}+\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1\). Hyperbola \(\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=\pm1\) with asymptotes \(y-k=\pm\frac ba(x-h)\). Parabola \(y=a(x-h)^2+k\).
☐Contour / cross-sectionA contour is \(f(x,y)=c\), drawn in the \(xy\)-plane. A cross-section fixes \(x=k\) or \(y=k\) and plots \(z\) against the other variable.
☐InterceptsSet \(y=0\) to get the \(x\)-intercepts and \(x=0\) to get the \(y\)-intercepts. "Label all intercepts" is worth marks.
☐ParametrisationsSegment: \(A+t(B-A)\), \(t\in[0,1]\). Circle: \((h+r\cos t,\ k+r\sin t)\). Ellipse: \((h+a\cos t,\ k+b\sin t)\).
☐Motion\(\mathbf v=\mathbf r'\), \(\mathbf a=\mathbf r''\), speed \(=\lVert\mathbf v\rVert\). Going backwards: \(\mathbf v=\int\mathbf a\,dt+\mathbf v_0\), \(\mathbf r=\int\mathbf v\,dt+\mathbf r_0\) (2025 S2 Q7, 2025 S1 Q7a).

Speed drill · "Which formula?" — 20 rapid prompts

Cover the answers. For each prompt, name the sheet item (or say "🧠 not on the sheet"), then get the answer. Aim for under 30 seconds to name the tool. All numbers are checked with SymPy.

1. Estimate \(\sqrt{4.02}\,(0.97)^2\) without a calculator. 📄 Item 1, linear approximation, with \(f=\sqrt x\,y^2\) at \((4,1)\): \(f=2\), \(f_x=\frac{y^2}{2\sqrt x}=\frac14\), \(f_y=2y\sqrt x=4\).
\(P=2+\tfrac14(0.02)+4(-0.03)=\) 1.885. (True value ≈ 1.8865.)
2. Rate of change of \(T=x^2y\) at \((1,2)\) in the direction of \((1,1)\). 📄 Item 2, directional derivative. \(\nabla T=(2xy,x^2)=(4,1)\), so \(D=\frac{(4,1)\cdot(1,1)}{\sqrt2}=\) \(\frac{5}{\sqrt2}=\frac{5\sqrt2}{2}\).
3. In which direction does \(f=xe^y\) increase fastest at \((1,0)\), and how fast? 🧠 Not on the sheet (gradient facts). The direction is \(\nabla f(1,0)=(e^y,xe^y)=\) \((1,1)\), and the rate is \(\lVert\nabla f\rVert=\) \(\sqrt2\).
4. Find \(dy/dx\) at \((1,2)\) on the curve \(x^2y+y^3=10\). 📄 Item 4, implicit differentiation. \(F=x^2y+y^3-10\), and \(F(1,2)=0\) ✓. \(F_x=2xy=4\), \(F_y=x^2+3y^2=13\), so \(y'=\) \(-\frac{4}{13}\).
5. Classify the critical point \((0,0)\) of \(f=x^2+4xy+y^2\). 📄 Item 5, Hessian, plus the 🧠 classification table. \(f_{xx}=2,\ f_{xy}=4,\ f_{yy}=2\), so \(D=4-16=-12\lt0\): a saddle point.
6. Maximise \(xyz\) subject to \(x+y+z=12\), with \(x,y,z\gt0\). 📄 Item 6, Lagrange. \(yz=xz=xy=\lambda\), so (all positive) \(x=y=z=4\), and the max is 64.
7. Work done by \(\mathbf F=(2x,2y)\) along \(\mathbf r(t)=(\cos t,\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le\pi\). 📄 Item 7 works, but 🧠 the potential is faster: \(\mathbf F=\nabla(x^2+y^2)\), so \(W=f(-1,0)-f(1,0)=1-1=\) 0. (Directly: \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=-2\cos t\sin t+2\sin t\cos t=0\).)
8. Length of \(\mathbf r(t)=\big(t^2,\ \tfrac23t^3\big)\) for \(0\le t\le\sqrt3\). 📄 Item 8, arc length. \(\lVert\mathbf r'\rVert=\sqrt{4t^2+4t^4}=2t\sqrt{1+t^2}\), so \(L=\big[\tfrac23(1+t^2)^{3/2}\big]_0^{\sqrt3}=\tfrac23(8-1)=\) \(\frac{14}{3}\).
9. \(z=\ln(x^2+y^2)\), \(x=e^t\), \(y=e^{-t}\). Find \(dz/dt\) at \(t=0\). 📄 Item 3, chain rule. At \(t=0\): \((x,y)=(1,1)\), \(z_x=\frac{2x}{x^2+y^2}=1\), \(z_y=1\), \(x'=1\), \(y'=-1\), so \(dz/dt=1-1=\) 0.
10. Tangent plane to \(z=x^2+y^2\) at \((1,2)\). 📄 Item 1, \(P\) is the tangent plane. \(z=5+2(x-1)+4(y-2)\), i.e. \(z=2x+4y-5\).
11. Is \(\mathbf F=(y\cos x,\ \sin x+2y)\) conservative? If so, find a potential. 🧠 Not on the sheet. \(P_y=\cos x=Q_x\) ✓, so it is conservative on \(\mathbb R^2\). \(f=\int y\cos x\,dx=y\sin x+h(y)\). Then \(f_y=\sin x+h'(y)=\sin x+2y\), so \(h=y^2\). Potential: \(f=y\sin x+y^2\).
12. Euler's method, \(y'=y\), \(y(0)=1\), step \(h=0.5\). Estimate \(y(1)\). 🧠 Not on the sheet. \(y_1=1+0.5(1)=1.5\), \(y_2=1.5+0.5(1.5)=\) 2.25. (Exact \(e\approx2.718\). Euler underestimates here because the solution is concave up.)
13. Solve \(y'+2y=4\), \(y(0)=0\). 🧠 Not on the sheet (integrating factor or separable). \(I=e^{2t}\), \((e^{2t}y)'=4e^{2t}\), so \(y=2+Ce^{-2t}\). The IC gives \(C=-2\): \(y=2-2e^{-2t}\).
14. General solution of \(y''-4y'+4y=0\). 🧠 Not on the sheet. \(\lambda^2-4\lambda+4=(\lambda-2)^2\) is a repeated root, so \(y=(c_1+c_2t)e^{2t}\).
15. Show \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{x^2y}{x^4+y^2}\) does not exist. 🧠 Not on the sheet (two-path test). Along \(y=mx\): \(\frac{mx^3}{x^4+m^2x^2}=\frac{mx}{x^2+m^2}\to0\). Along \(y=x^2\): \(\frac{x^4}{2x^4}=\frac12\). Since \(0\neq\frac12\), the limit DNE.
16. \(V=\pi r^2h\). Estimate the change in \(V\) when \(r\) goes from 3 to 3.01 and \(h\) from 5 to 4.98. 📄 Item 1 (linear approximation, small-change form): \(\Delta V\approx V_r\Delta r+V_h\Delta h=2\pi rh(0.01)+\pi r^2(-0.02)=0.3\pi-0.18\pi=\) \(0.12\pi\).
17. Quadratic approximation of \(f=e^x\cos y\) at \((0,0)\). 📄 Item 1, \(Q\). \(f=1,\ f_x=1,\ f_y=0,\ f_{xx}=1,\ f_{xy}=0,\ f_{yy}=-1\), so \(Q=1+x+\tfrac12x^2-\tfrac12y^2\).
18. Global max and min of \(f=x^2-y^2\) on the square \([-1,1]\times[-1,1]\). 🧠 Not on the sheet (closed-region method). The only interior critical point is \((0,0)\), where \(f=0\) (a saddle, by item 5). On the boundary, \(x=\pm1\) gives \(1-y^2\in[0,1]\) and \(y=\pm1\) gives \(x^2-1\in[-1,0]\). So the max is 1 at \((\pm1,0)\) and the min is −1 at \((0,\pm1)\).
19. \(f_x=x^2y\), \(f_y=x^2\), and \(x=u^2\), \(y=uv^2\). Find \(\partial f/\partial u\). 📄 Item 3, chain rule, upgraded to partials (the 2024 S2 Q6 style, with fresh numbers): \(f_u=f_x x_u+f_y y_u=x^2y(2u)+x^2(v^2)\).
Substituting, \(f_u=u^4\cdot uv^2\cdot2u+u^4v^2=\) \(2u^6v^2+u^4v^2\).
20. \(\mathbf a=(0,0,-10)\), \(\mathbf v(0)=(1,2,0)\), \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0,5)\). When does it hit \(z=0\), and where? 🧠 Not on the sheet (integrate twice). \(\mathbf v=(1,2,-10t)\) and \(\mathbf r=(t,\ 2t,\ 5-5t^2)\). \(z=0\) gives \(t=1\), at \((1,2,0)\).
