§1 · Concepts in depth

This chapter leans on single-variable integration (substitution, integration by parts, \(\int\sec t\,dt\)), trig identities (\(\sin^2+\cos^2=1\), double angles) and dot products. If any of those feel shaky, do Ground Floor · integrals, · trig and · vectors first.

🧭 The big picture: a curve is a movie, not a picture📘 Workbook §5.1

Until now a curve was a picture: a set of points satisfying an equation like \(x^2+y^2=4\). A parametrisation turns the picture into a movie. It tells you where a particle is at each instant \(t\):

$$\mathbf r(t)=\big(x(t),\,y(t),\,z(t)\big),\qquad a\le t\le b .$$

The movie carries information the picture doesn't:

  • a start and an end: \(\mathbf r(a)\) and \(\mathbf r(b)\);
  • a direction of travel (orientation): clockwise or anticlockwise, left to right or right to left;
  • a speed: how fast the particle covers the ground;
  • how many times the curve is traced. \(t\in[0,4\pi]\) goes round a circle twice.

One picture has infinitely many movies. \((\cos t,\sin t)\), \((\cos 2t,\sin 2t)\) and \((\cos t,-\sin t)\) all draw the unit circle, but at different speeds and in different directions. Exam questions test exactly these differences: "trace out clockwise starting from…", "indicate the starting point and the direction of travel", "the length of the path travelled".

The course summary's direction rule is back-to-front. The MATH1052 summary (p 6) says \(x=r\cos(\omega t+\phi)+h,\ y=r\sin(\omega t+\phi)+k\) is "clockwise if \(\omega\gt0\)". That is wrong. With \(\omega\gt0\), at \(t=0,\phi=0\) the particle is at the rightmost point \((h+r,k)\) with velocity \((0,r\omega)\), i.e. moving up, so the motion is anticlockwise. (Tutorial 11 Q2(a) agrees: clockwise needs \(\cos(-t),\sin(-t)\).) Don't memorise a rule. Check with \(t=0\) and \(t=\pi/2\).

🧭 Going backwards: eliminating the parameter📘 Workbook §5.1.1

Exams often hand you \(\mathbf r(t)\) and ask "what path is this?" or "sketch the path". The trick is to find a relation between \(x\) and \(y\) with no \(t\) in it:

  • Trig pairs: isolate \(\cos t\) and \(\sin t\), then use \(\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1\). \(x=1+2\cos t,\ y=-3+5\sin t \Rightarrow \frac{(x-1)^2}{4}+\frac{(y+3)^2}{25}=1\).
  • Double angles: 2023 S1 Q7 has \(x=16\sin\pi t,\ y=4\cos 2\pi t\). Use \(\cos2\theta=1-2\sin^2\theta\) to get \(y=4-\frac{x^2}{32}\), a parabola.
  • Polynomials: solve one coordinate for \(t\) and substitute. \(y=2t\Rightarrow t=y/2\), then \(x=-4t^2+8t+1=5-(y-2)^2\) (2023 S2 Q6).
  • Polar-looking: \(x^2+y^2\) often simplifies. For \((t\cos t,\,t\sin t)\), \(x^2+y^2=t^2\), so the distance from the origin equals \(t\): a spiral (2024 S1/S2 Q8). For \((e^t\sin t,e^t\cos t)\), the radius is \(e^t\) (2022 S2 Q9).

Eliminating \(t\) can lose information. \((e^t,e^{2t})\) satisfies \(y=x^2\) but only traces the half with \(x\gt0\). \((\cos^2t,\sin^2t)\) satisfies \(x+y=1\) but stays inside the segment \(0\le x\le1\), and it runs back and forth. State the actual portion traced, the start, the end and the direction.

🧭 Position → velocity → acceleration (and back)📘 Workbook §5.2

Differentiate component by component:

$$\mathbf v(t)=\mathbf r'(t)=\big(x'(t),y'(t),z'(t)\big),\qquad \mathbf a(t)=\mathbf v'(t)=\mathbf r''(t).$$
  • Velocity \(\mathbf v\) is a vector. It is tangent to the path and points in the direction of motion.
  • Speed \(\|\mathbf v\|=\sqrt{x'^2+y'^2+z'^2}\) is a scalar, always \(\ge 0\). "Find the speed" wants a number or a function, not a vector.
  • Acceleration \(\mathbf a\) is a vector. Constant speed does not mean zero acceleration: uniform circular motion has \(\mathbf a\) pointing at the centre.

Going backwards (the projectile/diver question) means integrating twice. Each integration creates a constant vector, and the initial conditions pin it down:

$$\mathbf v(t)=\mathbf v(0)+\int_0^t\mathbf a(s)\,ds,\qquad \mathbf r(t)=\mathbf r(0)+\int_0^t\mathbf v(s)\,ds .$$

For constant gravity \(\mathbf a=(0,0,-g)\) this gives the formula worth memorising (and deriving in the exam):

$$\mathbf r(t)=\mathbf r_0+\mathbf v_0\,t+\tfrac12\mathbf a\,t^2=\big(x_0+u_1t,\ y_0+u_2t,\ z_0+u_3t-\tfrac{g}{2}t^2\big).$$

Markers give marks for showing the integration and the constants. The 2025 S2 official solution writes \(\mathbf v(t)=(c_1,c_2,-9.8t+c_3)\), then "as \(\mathbf v(0)=(2,0,0)\Rightarrow c_1=2,c_2=0,c_3=0\)". Copy that shape. Don't just quote the final formula.

🧭 Arc length: add up speed × time📘 Workbook §5.4.1

Distance = speed × time when speed is constant. When it varies, chop time into tiny pieces and add: \(\Delta s\approx\|\mathbf r'(t)\|\Delta t\). In the limit:

$$L=\int_a^b\|\mathbf r'(t)\|\,dt=\int_a^b\sqrt{x'(t)^2+y'(t)^2+z'(t)^2}\;dt\qquad(\text{on the formula sheet}).$$

For a graph \(y=g(x)\) (put \(x=t\)) this becomes \(L=\int_a^b\sqrt{1+g'(x)^2}\,dx\).

The exam secret: the square root always collapses. With no calculator, examiners pick curves where \(\|\mathbf r'\|^2\) is a perfect square or a constant. Look for:

  • \(\sin^2+\cos^2\): circles and helices give a constant speed. \((\cos3t,\sin3t,1+4t)\) has \(\|\mathbf r'\|=\sqrt{9+16}=5\).
  • cross terms cancelling: \((t^2\cos t,t^2\sin t,16-2t)\) gives \(t^4+4t^2+4=(t^2+2)^2\) (2024 S1 Q7).
  • "\(1+(\text{something})^2\)" completing to a square: \(1+(A-B)^2=(A+B)^2\) whenever \(4AB=1\). Example: \(1+t^2(t^2+2)=(t^2+1)^2\) (T11 Q4b).
  • half-angle: \(2-2\cos t=4\sin^2(t/2)\) (the cardioid, 2021 S1 Q9).
  • exponential factor: \(e^{-t}(\cos t,\sin t,1)\) gives \(\|\mathbf r'\|^2=3e^{-2t}\) (2023 S2 Q5).

\(\sqrt{A^2}=|A|\), not \(A\). If the speed is \(3|\sin t\cos t|\) you must split the interval where the sign changes, or use symmetry. Otherwise a whole astroid can come out as length \(0\).

🧭 Vector fields and work📘 Workbook §5.4.3

A vector field \(\mathbf F(x,y)=\big(P(x,y),Q(x,y)\big)\) attaches an arrow to every point: a force, a wind or a current. When a particle moves along \(C\), the field pushes it. The work done adds up "force component along the direction of motion × distance":

$$W=\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_a^b\mathbf F\big(\mathbf r(t)\big)\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\qquad(\text{on the formula sheet}).$$

Why the dot product? Only the part of the force along the motion does work. A force perpendicular to the path (like the string's pull on a ball on a string) does none. For a constant force along a straight segment this reduces to the school formula \(W=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf d\).

The summary's "Bop It" routine (direct computation):
  1. Par it: parametrise the path \(\mathbf r(t)\), \(a\le t\le b\), in the right direction.
  2. Diff it: find \(\mathbf r'(t)\).
  3. F in t: substitute \(x=x(t),y=y(t),(z=z(t))\) into \(\mathbf F\) to get \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\).
  4. Dot it: form \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\), a single function of \(t\).
  5. Int it: \(W=\int_a^b(\dots)\,dt\).

Direction matters. Reversing the path negates the work: \(\int_{-C}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=-\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\). Arc length doesn't care about direction. Work does.

🧭 Conservative fields: when the path doesn't matter📘 Workbook §5.5

Some fields are the gradient of a scalar function: \(\mathbf F=\nabla f=(f_x,f_y)\). Then \(f\) is called a potential, and \(\mathbf F\) is conservative (also called a gradient field). For these fields, work behaves like height change on a hill: only the start and end points matter.

Fundamental theorem for line integrals. If \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\) and \(C\) runs from \(A\) to \(B\), then

$$\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=f(B)-f(A).$$

Why: by the chain rule (Ch 5), \(\frac{d}{dt}f(\mathbf r(t))=f_x x'+f_y y'=\nabla f\cdot\mathbf r'\). So the work integrand is an exact derivative, and \(\int_a^b\frac{d}{dt}f(\mathbf r(t))\,dt=f(\mathbf r(b))-f(\mathbf r(a))\).

Three consequences examiners exploit:

  1. Path independence. Any two paths from \(A\) to \(B\) give the same work. So a horrible path like \(x=t\cos(\pi t),\ y=2t^2\sin(\pi t)\) (2025 S2 Q8) needs only its endpoints.
  2. Closed loops give zero. If \(A=B\) then \(W=f(A)-f(A)=0\).
  3. Contrapositive test. If some closed loop has nonzero work, or two paths give different work, then \(\mathbf F\) is not conservative.

🧭 The test: \(P_y=Q_x\) (and curl in 3-D)📘 Workbook §5.5.1

If \(\mathbf F=(P,Q)=(f_x,f_y)\), then \(P_y=f_{xy}\) and \(Q_x=f_{yx}\). By Clairaut (Ch 4) these are equal. So:

$$\mathbf F=(P,Q)\ \text{conservative}\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}.$$

MATH1052 uses the converse too: if \(P_y=Q_x\) everywhere on \(\mathbb R^2\) (or any region with no holes), then \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. This is the "show that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative" routine in every paper. (The 2021 S1 solutions note there is a "simply connected domain" subtlety, but that is MATH2001 material, not tested here.)

3-D version (2020 S1 Q9 and 2023 S1 Q8 use 3-D fields). \(\mathbf F=(P,Q,R)\) is conservative on \(\mathbb R^3\) iff all three cross-partials match:

$$P_y=Q_x,\qquad P_z=R_x,\qquad Q_z=R_y\qquad(\text{i.e. }\nabla\times\mathbf F=\mathbf 0).$$

🧭 Building the potential: partial integration📘 Workbook §5.5

  1. Integrate \(P\) with respect to \(x\), treating \(y\) as a constant: \(f=\int P\,dx+g(y)\). The "constant" is an unknown function of \(y\).
  2. Differentiate that \(f\) with respect to \(y\) and set it equal to \(Q\).
  3. Everything except \(g'(y)\) must cancel. Solve for \(g'(y)\) (it depends on \(y\) only), then integrate.
  4. State \(f\) (take the constant \(=0\)), and check \(\nabla f=\mathbf F\).
In 3-D, add one more round: \(f=\int P\,dx+g(y,z)\). Match \(f_y=Q\) to find \(g_y\) and integrate to get \(g=h(y)+k(z)\)-type pieces. Then match \(f_z=R\).

If \(g'(y)\) turns out to depend on \(x\), you made an algebra slip or the field is not conservative. Never "fix" it by pretending \(x\) is constant.

§2 · Key methods & when to use them

The 2022–2025 formula sheets print only \(W=\int_a^b\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\) and \(L=\int_a^b\|\mathbf r'(t)\|\,dt\). Everything else on this page (templates, the \(P_y=Q_x\) test, partial integration, the fundamental theorem) you must bring in your head.

M1 · Parametrising a segment or line (not on sheet)📘 Workbook §5.2.2

$$\mathbf r(t)=P+t\,(Q-P),\quad 0\le t\le 1\qquad\text{or}\qquad \mathbf r(t)=\mathbf r_0+t\mathbf v,\ t\in\mathbb R.$$
"Straight line from \(A\) to \(B\)", "line through … in the direction of …", or the straight-line leg of a composite path (2025 S1 Q8(e): the straight line from \((1,0)\) to \((-1,0)\)).

Mini-example. From \((1,0)\) to \((-1,0)\): \(\mathbf r(t)=(1,0)+t(-2,0)=(1-2t,\,0)\), \(0\le t\le1\). Check: \(\mathbf r(0)=(1,0)\) ✓ and \(\mathbf r(1)=(-1,0)\) ✓.

M2 · Parametrising circles and ellipses with a given start and direction📘 Workbook §5.1.2

$$\text{anticlockwise from the right: }(h+a\cos t,\ k+b\sin t);\qquad\text{clockwise from the right: }(h+a\cos t,\ k-b\sin t).$$
"Circle of radius … centred at … traced clockwise starting from …", "ellipse crossing the axes at …", "half of the ellipse", "one revolution".

Recipe: (1) put the curve in standard form, completing the square if needed (Ch 3); (2) read off \(h,k,a,b\); (3) choose \(\sin\)/\(\cos\) and signs so that \(t=0\) is the required start; (4) check the direction at \(t=\pi/2\); (5) state the \(t\)-range (\(2\pi\) for one lap, \(\pi\) for half).

M3 · Velocity, acceleration, speed📘 Workbook §5.2.3–5.2.4

$$\mathbf v=\mathbf r',\qquad \mathbf a=\mathbf r'',\qquad \text{speed}=\|\mathbf r'\|.$$
"Find the velocity / acceleration / speed at time \(t\) (or at \(t=\dots\))", "show the speed is constant", "velocity parallel to the line …" (then \(\mathbf v=k\,\mathbf d\)).

Mini-example (2024 S2 Q7). \(\mathbf r=(\cos\frac{\pi t}4-1,\ \sin\frac{\pi t}4)\), so \(\mathbf v=\left(-\frac\pi4\sin\frac{\pi t}4,\ \frac\pi4\cos\frac{\pi t}4\right)\) and the speed is \(\frac\pi4\).

M4 · Integrating acceleration with initial conditions📘 Workbook §5.2

$$\mathbf v(t)=\int\mathbf a\,dt+\mathbf c_1,\ \ \mathbf c_1\text{ from }\mathbf v(0);\qquad \mathbf r(t)=\int\mathbf v\,dt+\mathbf c_2,\ \ \mathbf c_2\text{ from }\mathbf r(0).$$
"A diver/ball/stone … initial velocity … acceleration due to gravity is …", "a particle has velocity \(\mathbf v(t)=\dots\) and starts at …". Follow-ups: "when does it hit the ground" (set \(z=0\), take the positive root), "where does it land" (substitute that time), "maximum height" (set \(v_z=0\)).

Mini-example. \(\mathbf a=(0,0,-10)\), \(\mathbf v_0=(4,3,10)\), \(\mathbf r_0=(0,0,15)\) gives \(\mathbf r=(4t,\,3t,\,15+10t-5t^2)\). It lands when \(5t^2-10t-15=0\iff(t-3)(t+1)=0\), so \(t=3\).

M5 · Arc length (on the sheet)📘 Workbook §5.4.1

$$L=\int_a^b\|\mathbf r'(t)\|\,dt,\qquad\text{graph: }L=\int_a^b\sqrt{1+g'(x)^2}\,dx.$$
"Length of the curve", "distance travelled", "how far does it travel in … seconds", "find \(a\) so that the helix has length 20".

Recipe: differentiate → square and add → expand and look for \(\sin^2+\cos^2\) or a perfect square → take the root (with \(|\cdot|\)) → integrate. If the speed is constant \(c\), then \(L=c\,(b-a)\) with no integral needed.

M6 · Work by direct computation (on the sheet)📘 Workbook §5.4.3, 5.6

$$W=\int_a^b\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt .$$
The field is not conservative (\(P_y\ne Q_x\)), or the question says "use the formula \(W=\int\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'\,dt\)" (2023 S1 Q8(b), 2025 S1 Q8(e)ii), or the path is a closed loop of a non-conservative field.

Mini-example. \(\mathbf F=(x-2y,3x+y)\) once anticlockwise round \(x^2+y^2=4\): the integrand is \(8\sin^2t+12\cos^2t\), so \(W=8\pi+12\pi=20\pi\ne0\). The field is not conservative.

M7 · Conservative test📘 Workbook §5.5.1

$$\text{2-D: } \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x};\qquad\text{3-D: } P_y=Q_x,\ P_z=R_x,\ Q_z=R_y.$$
"Show that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative", "is \(\mathbf F\) a gradient field? justify", "determine \(A\) and \(B\) so that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative" (match coefficients of the polynomial identity \(P_y\equiv Q_x\)).

Marker's statement (2024 S2 MS gives 2 marks just for it): "A vector field \(\mathbf F=P\mathbf i+Q\mathbf j\) is conservative if \(\partial P/\partial y=\partial Q/\partial x\)." Write it, compute both sides, compare, conclude.

M8 · Potential by partial integration, then the fundamental theorem📘 Workbook §5.5

$$f=\int P\,dx+g(y),\quad f_y=Q\Rightarrow g'(y);\qquad W=f(\mathbf r(b))-f(\mathbf r(a)).$$
After showing \(\mathbf F\) is conservative, "find a potential \(f\) with \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\)", then "compute the work along [horrible path]". Evaluate \(\mathbf r\) at the two endpoint times and plug into \(f\).

Mini-example (2025 S2 Q8). \(\mathbf F=(2x\cos y,-x^2\sin y)\), so \(f=x^2\cos y\). The path has \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0)\) and \(\mathbf r(3)=(3\cos3\pi,\,18\sin3\pi)=(-3,0)\), so \(W=9\cos0-0=9\).

Example 1 — Three parametrisations with prescribed start and direction

Q. Write a parametrisation, with a \(t\)-range, for each curve. [6 marks]

  • (a) The line segment from \((1,-2)\) to \((4,2)\).
  • (b) The circle of radius 2 centred at \((1,3)\), traced once clockwise starting from \((3,3)\).
  • (c) The ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{4}=1\), traced once anticlockwise starting from \((0,2)\).

(a) The direction vector is \(Q-P=(4,2)-(1,-2)=(3,4)\), so

$$\mathbf r(t)=(1,-2)+t(3,4)=(1+3t,\ -2+4t),\qquad 0\le t\le1.$$

Check: \(\mathbf r(0)=(1,-2)\) ✓ and \(\mathbf r(1)=(4,2)\) ✓. (The segment has length \(\|(3,4)\|=5\).)

(b) The start \((3,3)=(1+2,\,3)\) is the rightmost point, so use \(\cos\) in \(x\). For clockwise motion the particle must move down from the rightmost point, so use \(-\sin\) in \(y\):

$$\mathbf r(t)=(1+2\cos t,\ 3-2\sin t),\qquad 0\le t\le 2\pi.$$

Check: \(\mathbf r(0)=(3,3)\) ✓. \(\mathbf r'(0)=(0,-2)\), pointing down, so the motion is clockwise ✓. \((x-1)^2+(y-3)^2=4\cos^2t+4\sin^2t=4\) ✓.

(c) Here \(a=3\) (along \(x\)) and \(b=2\) (along \(y\)). The start \((0,2)\) is the top. Anticlockwise from the top means moving left:

$$\mathbf r(t)=(-3\sin t,\ 2\cos t),\qquad 0\le t\le2\pi.$$

Check: \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,2)\) ✓. \(\mathbf r(\pi/2)=(-3,0)\), so it goes top → left: anticlockwise ✓. \(\frac{9\sin^2t}{9}+\frac{4\cos^2t}{4}=1\) ✓.

Write the two checks (start point and quarter-turn) in the exam. They take 20 seconds and prove to the marker the orientation is deliberate, not luck.

Example 2 — Projectile: integrate a → v → r, then landing time, max height, impact speed

Q. A ball is thrown from the point \((0,0,15)\) (metres) with initial velocity \(\mathbf v_0=(4,3,10)\) m/s. Take the acceleration to be \(\mathbf a=(0,0,-10)\) m/s².

  • (a) Find the velocity and position vectors at time \(t\). [5 marks]
  • (b) When and where does the ball hit the ground \(z=0\)? [4 marks]
  • (c) Find the maximum height and the speed on impact. [3 marks]

(a) Integrate the acceleration componentwise:

$$\mathbf v(t)=\int(0,0,-10)\,dt=(c_1,\ c_2,\ -10t+c_3).$$

From \(\mathbf v(0)=(4,3,10)\) we get \(c_1=4,\ c_2=3,\ c_3=10\), so \(\boxed{\mathbf v(t)=(4,\ 3,\ 10-10t)}\). Integrate again:

$$\mathbf r(t)=(4t+c_4,\ 3t+c_5,\ 10t-5t^2+c_6).$$

From \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0,15)\) we get \(c_4=c_5=0,\ c_6=15\), so \(\boxed{\mathbf r(t)=(4t,\ 3t,\ 15+10t-5t^2)}\).

(b) The ball hits the ground when \(z=0\): \(15+10t-5t^2=0\iff t^2-2t-3=0\iff(t-3)(t+1)=0\). Time must be positive, so \(t=3\) s, and the ball lands at \(\mathbf r(3)=(12,\,9,\,0)\).

(c) Maximum height is when \(v_z=10-10t=0\), i.e. \(t=1\): \(z(1)=15+10-5=20\) m. On impact \(\mathbf v(3)=(4,3,-20)\), so the speed is \(\sqrt{16+9+400}=\sqrt{425}=5\sqrt{17}\) m/s.

Reject the negative root \(t=-1\) explicitly ("\(t\ge0\)"). Speed is the magnitude, a positive number. Don't give \((4,3,-20)\) as "the speed".

Example 3 — Arc length where the square root collapses

Q. Find the length of \(\mathbf r(t)=(6t,\ 3\sqrt2\,t^2,\ 2t^3)\) for \(0\le t\le1\). [5 marks]

$$\mathbf r'(t)=(6,\ 6\sqrt2\,t,\ 6t^2),\qquad \|\mathbf r'\|^2=36+72t^2+36t^4=36(1+2t^2+t^4)=36(1+t^2)^2.$$

So \(\|\mathbf r'(t)\|=6(1+t^2)\), which is positive, so no absolute value issue. Then

$$L=\int_0^1 6(1+t^2)\,dt=6\left[t+\tfrac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^1=6\cdot\tfrac43=\boxed{8}.$$

The coefficients \(6,\,6\sqrt2,\,6\) were chosen so that \(36+72t^2+36t^4\) is \(36(1+t^2)^2\). When you see a middle coefficient that is exactly twice the geometric mean of the outer two, spot the square immediately.

Example 4 — Helix: constant speed, length without integrating

Q. Find the speed of \(\mathbf r(t)=(3\cos t,\ 3\sin t,\ 4t)\) and the length of one full turn \(0\le t\le2\pi\). [4 marks]

$$\mathbf r'=(-3\sin t,\ 3\cos t,\ 4),\qquad\|\mathbf r'\|=\sqrt{9\sin^2t+9\cos^2t+16}=\sqrt{25}=5.$$

The speed is constant at 5, so \(L=\int_0^{2\pi}5\,dt=10\pi\).

Same idea as 2021 S2 Q4 ("find \(a\) such that the helix has length 20"): once the speed is a constant \(c\), \(L=c\cdot a\), so you solve \(c\,a=20\).

Example 5 — Work by direct computation along two different paths (non-conservative)

Q. Let \(\mathbf F(x,y)=(xy,\ x-y)\). (a) Compute the work done moving a particle from \((0,0)\) to \((1,1)\) along the parabola \(y=x^2\). (b) Repeat along the straight line \(y=x\). (c) What do you conclude? [8 marks]

(a) Par it: \(\mathbf r(t)=(t,t^2)\), \(0\le t\le1\). Diff it: \(\mathbf r'=(1,2t)\). F in t: \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))=(t\cdot t^2,\ t-t^2)=(t^3,\ t-t^2)\). Dot it: \(t^3+2t(t-t^2)=2t^2-t^3\). Int it:

$$W_1=\int_0^1(2t^2-t^3)\,dt=\tfrac23-\tfrac14=\boxed{\tfrac{5}{12}}.$$

(b) \(\mathbf r(t)=(t,t)\), \(\mathbf r'=(1,1)\), \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))=(t^2,0)\), so \(W_2=\int_0^1t^2\,dt=\boxed{\tfrac13}\).

(c) Same endpoints, different work, so the work is path-dependent and \(\mathbf F\) is not conservative. This agrees with the test: \(P_y=x\) while \(Q_x=1\), and these are not equal.

Example 6 — Conservative field on a nasty path (the fundamental theorem)

Q. Let \(\mathbf F(x,y)=(2xy+e^x,\ x^2+3y^2)\). (a) Show \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. (b) Find a potential. (c) Find the work done by \(\mathbf F\) along \(\mathbf r(t)=\big(t\cos(2\pi t),\ t^2+\sin(\pi t)\big)\), \(0\le t\le1\). [14 marks]

(a) A field \(\mathbf F=P\mathbf i+Q\mathbf j\) on \(\mathbb R^2\) is conservative if \(P_y=Q_x\). Here \(P_y=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(2xy+e^x)=2x\) and \(Q_x=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2+3y^2)=2x\). They are equal, so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.

(b) We need \(f_x=2xy+e^x\) and \(f_y=x^2+3y^2\). Integrating the first with respect to \(x\):

$$f(x,y)=x^2y+e^x+g(y).$$

Then \(f_y=x^2+g'(y)\). Setting this equal to \(x^2+3y^2\) gives \(g'(y)=3y^2\), so \(g(y)=y^3\) (constant taken as 0). Hence \(\boxed{f(x,y)=x^2y+e^x+y^3}\). Check: \(\nabla f=(2xy+e^x,\ x^2+3y^2)=\mathbf F\) ✓.

(c) Since \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\), the work depends only on the endpoints. \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,\,0)\) and \(\mathbf r(1)=(\cos2\pi,\ 1+\sin\pi)=(1,1)\). So

$$W=f(1,1)-f(0,0)=(1+e+1)-(0+1+0)=\boxed{e+1}.$$

The direct integral \(\int_0^1\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\) here is a monster (a product of \(\cos 2\pi t\), \(e^{t\cos2\pi t}\), …). SymPy's numerical integration gives \(3.71828\ldots=e+1\), confirming the answer. The examiner designed the path to be unintegrable by hand so that you use path independence.

Example 7 — Work around a closed loop of a non-conservative field

Q. Compute the work done by \(\mathbf F=(x-2y,\ 3x+y)\) moving a particle once anticlockwise around the circle \(x^2+y^2=4\). Is \(\mathbf F\) conservative? [6 marks]

Par it: \(\mathbf r(t)=(2\cos t,\,2\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\). Then \(\mathbf r'=(-2\sin t,\,2\cos t)\) and \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))=(2\cos t-4\sin t,\ 6\cos t+2\sin t)\).

$$\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=-4\sin t\cos t+8\sin^2t+12\cos^2t+4\sin t\cos t=8\sin^2t+12\cos^2t.$$ $$W=\int_0^{2\pi}(8\sin^2t+12\cos^2t)\,dt=8\pi+12\pi=\boxed{20\pi}.$$

(We used \(\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2t\,dt=\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2t\,dt=\pi\).) The curve is closed but \(W\ne0\), so \(\mathbf F\) is not conservative. The test agrees: \(P_y=-2\ne3=Q_x\).

Example 8 — A 3-D conservative field along a helix

Q. Let \(\mathbf F(x,y,z)=(yz,\ xz+2y,\ xy+1)\) and \(C:\ \mathbf r(t)=(\cos t,\sin t,t/\pi)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\). Show \(\mathbf F\) is conservative, find a potential, and find the work along \(C\). [10 marks]

Test (all three pairs): \(P_y=z=Q_x\) ✓, \(P_z=y=R_x\) ✓, \(Q_z=x=R_y\) ✓. So \(\mathbf F\) is conservative on \(\mathbb R^3\).

Potential. \(f=\int yz\,dx=xyz+g(y,z)\). Then \(f_y=xz+g_y=xz+2y\Rightarrow g_y=2y\Rightarrow g=y^2+h(z)\). Then \(f_z=xy+h'(z)=xy+1\Rightarrow h(z)=z\). So \(\boxed{f=xyz+y^2+z}\).

Work. \(\mathbf r(0)=(1,0,0)\) and \(\mathbf r(2\pi)=(1,0,2)\), so \(W=f(1,0,2)-f(1,0,0)=2-0=\boxed{2}\). (The direct integral also gives 2. Verified.)

Example 9 — Unknown constants that make a field conservative

Q. Let \(\mathbf F=(3x^2y+Ay^2,\ Bx^3+4xy+1)\). (a) Find \(A,B\) so that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. (b) For these values, find a potential and the work done along any path from \((1,0)\) to \((2,1)\). [12 marks]

(a) \(P_y=3x^2+2Ay\) and \(Q_x=3Bx^2+4y\). For \(P_y=Q_x\) to hold for all \((x,y)\), match coefficients: \(x^2\): \(3=3B\Rightarrow B=1\); \(y\): \(2A=4\Rightarrow A=2\).

(b) \(f=\int(3x^2y+2y^2)\,dx=x^3y+2xy^2+g(y)\). Then \(f_y=x^3+4xy+g'(y)=x^3+4xy+1\Rightarrow g=y\). So \(f=x^3y+2xy^2+y\) and

$$W=f(2,1)-f(1,0)=(8+4+1)-0=\boxed{13}.$$

"Match coefficients" is the justification. Write "since this must hold for all \(x,y\)". A marker won't accept \(A=2\) from plugging in a single point.

Type A — The conservative-field finale 📘 Workbook §5.5flagship · 13–18 marks

Looks like: "Consider the force field \(\mathbf F(x,y)=\dots\). (a) Show that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative [or: find \(A,B\) so that it is]. (b) Determine a potential function \(f\) such that \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\). (c) Calculate the work done … along the path [an ugly parametrisation]."

Routine (and where the marks sit, per the 2024 S2 marking scheme):
  1. State the criterion "\(\mathbf F=P\mathbf i+Q\mathbf j\) is conservative if \(P_y=Q_x\)" (2 marks for the statement alone).
  2. Compute \(P_y\) and \(Q_x\). Compare them, or match coefficients to find constants (3 marks). Write the resulting field out (1 mark).
  3. Potential: \(f=\int P\,dx+g(y)\). Differentiate, match with \(Q\), solve for \(g\) (5 marks working, 1 mark correct \(f\)).
  4. Work: "since \(\mathbf F\) is conservative, \(W=f(\mathbf r(b))-f(\mathbf r(a))\)". Compute both endpoints (3 marks), then evaluate (3 marks).

📄 Real exam question — 2025 S2 Q8 [18 marks]

Consider the force field

$$\mathbf F(x,y)=(2x\cos y,\ -x^2\sin y).$$
  • (a) (5 marks) Show that the force field \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.
  • (b) (7 marks) Determine a potential function \(f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) such that \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\).
  • (c) (6 marks) Calculate the work done by the force field \(\mathbf F\) when moving a particle along the path defined by $$x(t)=t\cos(\pi t),\qquad y(t)=2t^2\sin(\pi t),\qquad 0\le t\le3.$$
Full worked solution — 2025 S2 Q8(a)–(c)

(a) Write \(\mathbf F=(P,Q)\) with \(P=2x\cos y\) and \(Q=-x^2\sin y\). A vector field on \(\mathbb R^2\) is conservative if \(\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}=\dfrac{\partial Q}{\partial x}\). We have

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}=-2x\sin y,\qquad \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}=-2x\sin y.$$

These are equal for all \((x,y)\in\mathbb R^2\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.

(b) We need \(f_x=2x\cos y\) and \(f_y=-x^2\sin y\). Integrating the first with respect to \(x\):

$$f(x,y)=\int2x\cos y\,dx=x^2\cos y+\varphi(y).$$

Then \(f_y=-x^2\sin y+\varphi'(y)\). Setting this equal to \(-x^2\sin y\) gives \(\varphi'(y)=0\), so \(\varphi(y)=C\). Taking \(C=0\): \(\boxed{f(x,y)=x^2\cos y}\).

(c) Since \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\) is conservative, the work is path-independent: \(W=\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=f(\mathbf r(3))-f(\mathbf r(0))\). The endpoints are

$$\mathbf r(0)=(0,0),\qquad \mathbf r(3)=\big(3\cos3\pi,\ 18\sin3\pi\big)=(-3,\,0).$$ $$W=f(-3,0)-f(0,0)=(-3)^2\cos0-0=\boxed{9}.$$

Do not try the direct integral. The official solution uses only the endpoints. Evaluate \(\cos3\pi=-1\) and \(\sin3\pi=0\) carefully. This is where people lose the marks.

Variant — 2024 S2 Q8 [18 marks]: \(\mathbf F=(5x^2y+Ay^2+xy^2)\mathbf i+(Bx^3+3yx+yx^2)\mathbf j\), path \((t\cos t,t\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le\pi/2\). \(P_y=5x^2+2Ay+2xy\) and \(Q_x=3Bx^2+3y+2xy\), so \(B=\frac53\) and \(A=\frac32\). The potential is \(f=\frac53x^3y+\frac32xy^2+\frac12x^2y^2\). The endpoints are \((0,0)\) and \((0,\frac\pi2)\), and \(f\) vanishes at both (every term has a factor \(x\)), so \(W=0\).

Variant — 2023 S2 Q6 [14 marks]: \(\mathbf F=(4x^3y,x^4)=\nabla(x^4y)\) along \(x=-4t^2+8t+1,\ y=2t\), \(t\in[0,2]\). The path is \(x=5-(y-2)^2\), a leftward parabola with vertex \((5,2)\), from \((1,0)\) to \((1,4)\). \(W=f(1,4)-f(1,0)=4\).

Type B — Motion from acceleration or velocity 📘 Workbook §5.2~10–13 marks

Looks like: "A diver / particle / two bugs … initial position … initial velocity … acceleration \(\mathbf a=\dots\). Determine the position vector at time \(t\). When does it hit …? Where is it then?"

Routine: integrate componentwise with constants → fix the constants with \(\mathbf v(0)\) → integrate again → fix with \(\mathbf r(0)\) → box \(\mathbf r(t)\). Then solve the event equation (\(z=0\), distance \(=4\), …) for the first positive \(t\), and substitute back.

📄 Real exam question — 2025 S2 Q7 [12 marks]

A diver jumps from a cliff 40 metres above sea level, starting at position \((0,0,40)\). The diver pushes off horizontally with an initial velocity \(\vec v_0=(2,0,0)\), and the acceleration due to gravity is \(\vec a=(0,0,-9.8)\).

  • (a) (5 marks) Determine the position vector of the diver at time \(t\).
  • (b) (4 marks) Determine the time it takes for the diver to hit the water.
  • (c) (3 marks) Determine the diver's position at the moment they hit the water.

(The official solutions booklet prints the part marks as 5/3/2. The question paper, quoted here, has 5/4/3.)

Full worked solution — 2025 S2 Q7(a)–(c)

(a) \(\mathbf a=\dfrac{d\mathbf v}{dt}=(0,0,-9.8)\), so

$$\mathbf v(t)=\Big(\int0\,dt,\int0\,dt,\int-9.8\,dt\Big)=(c_1,\ c_2,\ -9.8t+c_3).$$

As \(\mathbf v(0)=(2,0,0)=(c_1,c_2,c_3)\), \(c_1=2,\ c_2=0,\ c_3=0\): \(\mathbf v(t)=(2,\,0,\,-9.8t)\). Next,

$$\mathbf r(t)=\Big(\int2\,dt,\int0\,dt,\int-9.8t\,dt\Big)=(2t+c_4,\ c_5,\ -4.9t^2+c_6).$$

As \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0,40)\), \(c_4=c_5=0\) and \(c_6=40\):

$$\boxed{\mathbf r(t)=(2t,\ 0,\ 40-4.9t^2)}.$$

(b) The diver hits the water when \(z=0\): \(4.9t^2=40\Rightarrow t^2=\dfrac{40}{4.9}=\dfrac{400}{49}\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{20}{7}\approx2.86\) s (taking \(t\gt0\)).

(c) \(\mathbf r\!\left(\tfrac{20}{7}\right)=\left(\tfrac{40}{7},\ 0,\ 40-4.9\cdot\tfrac{400}{49}\right)=\boxed{\left(\tfrac{40}{7},\,0,\,0\right)}\).

No calculator: turn \(4.9\) into \(\tfrac{49}{10}\) so that \(\tfrac{40}{4.9}=\tfrac{400}{49}\) is a perfect square over a perfect square. Examiners pick numbers like this on purpose.

Variant — 2025 S1 Q7 [13 marks]: \(\mathbf v_A=(6\sin3t,6\cos3t,0)\) from \((-2,0,2)\) gives \(\mathbf r_A=(-2\cos3t,\,2\sin3t,\,2)\). \(\mathbf v_B=(\cos t,-\sin t,0)\) from \((0,1,-1)\) gives \(\mathbf r_B=(\sin t,\,\cos t,\,-1)\). Then \(\|\mathbf r_A-\mathbf r_B\|^2=14-4\sin2t\) (using \(\sin(3t-t)\)). Distance 4 means \(\sin2t=-\tfrac12\), first at \(2t=\tfrac{7\pi}6\), so \(t=\tfrac{7\pi}{12}\) and \(\mathbf r_A=(-\sqrt2,-\sqrt2,2)\). 2020 S1 Q4: \(\mathbf v=(\cos t,-\sin t,t)\) from \((3,-2,3)\) gives \(\mathbf r=(3+\sin t,\ \cos t-3,\ 3+\tfrac{t^2}{2})\).

Type C — Speed and arc length of a given curve 📘 Workbook §5.4.1~10–13 marks

Looks like: "Determine the speed at \(t=\dots\)", "determine the distance travelled / the length of the path in \(0\le t\le\dots\)", "find \(a\) such that the helix has length 20", "show that the speed is constant".

Routine: \(\mathbf r'\) → \(\|\mathbf r'\|^2\) → expand, cancel the cross terms, use \(\sin^2+\cos^2=1\) → recognise a square → \(L=\int\|\mathbf r'\|\,dt\).

📄 Real exam question — 2024 S1 Q7 [13 marks]

A jacaranda flower tumbles to the ground along the curve

$$\mathbf r(t)=t^2\cos(t)\,\mathbf i+t^2\sin(t)\,\mathbf j+(16-2t)\,\mathbf k.$$
  • (a) (8 marks) Determine the speed of the jacaranda flower at \(t=\pi\).
  • (b) (5 marks) Determine the distance travelled by the jacaranda flower in the time interval \(0\le t\le2\).
Full worked solution — 2024 S1 Q7(a)–(b)

(a) Product rule on each component:

$$\mathbf r'(t)=\big(2t\cos t-t^2\sin t,\ \ 2t\sin t+t^2\cos t,\ \ -2\big).$$ $$\begin{aligned}\|\mathbf r'\|^2&=(4t^2\cos^2t-4t^3\sin t\cos t+t^4\sin^2t)+(4t^2\sin^2t+4t^3\sin t\cos t+t^4\cos^2t)+4\\&=4t^2+t^4+4=(t^2+2)^2.\end{aligned}$$

So the speed is \(\|\mathbf r'(t)\|=t^2+2\) (positive), and at \(t=\pi\) the speed is \(\boxed{\pi^2+2}\).

(b) \(L=\displaystyle\int_0^2(t^2+2)\,dt=\left[\tfrac{t^3}3+2t\right]_0^2=\tfrac83+4=\boxed{\tfrac{20}{3}}\).

Most of the 8 marks in (a) are for the expansion. Show the cancelling \(\pm4t^3\sin t\cos t\) terms explicitly. That is the line the official solution strikes through.

Other instances, one line each: 2024 S2 Q7: speed \(\frac\pi4\), \(L=\frac\pi2\). 2023 S2 Q5: \(\|\mathbf r'\|=\sqrt3e^{-t}\), \(L=\sqrt3(1-e^{-1})\). 2021 S2 Q4: speed 5, so \(5a=20\Rightarrow a=4\). 2021 S1 Q9: \(\|\mathbf r'\|^2=f^2+f'^2\); for the cardioid \(f=3(1-\cos t)\) this is \(36\sin^2\frac t2\), giving \(L=24\). 2022 S1 Q9: speed \(2\,|d\theta/dt|=6\), distance in 5 s is 30 m. 2020 S2 Q9: \(\|\mathbf r'\|=1+t^2\), \(L=\frac{14}3\). 2022 S2 Q8: speed on impact \(\sqrt3\). 2020 S1 Q6: circle distance \(2\pi R\omega\).

Type D — Identify, sketch or parametrise a path 📘 Workbook §5.1–5.33–13 marks (up to 10 for a sketch!)

Looks like: "Show the hexbug moves along a parabolic path; give the Cartesian equation", "sketch the path \(C\); indicate the starting point and the direction of travel", "find a parametric representation of this [conic]", "when does it first return to its starting point?"

Routine: eliminate \(t\) (trig identity or substitution) → name the curve → table a few \(t\)-values including the endpoints → sketch only the traced portion with an arrow and labelled start/end points.

📄 Real exam question — 2023 S1 Q7 [13 marks]

A hexbug moves on a table so that its position at time \(t\) is

$$\mathbf r(t)=16\sin(\pi t)\,\mathbf i+4\cos(2\pi t)\,\mathbf j.$$
  • (a) (4 marks) Find the velocity and acceleration of the hexbug at time \(t\).
  • (b) (5 marks) Show that the hexbug moves along a parabolic path. Give the cartesian equation of the path. Hint: \(\cos(2\theta)=1-2\sin^2\theta\).
  • (c) (4 marks) Determine when the hexbug first returns to its starting point.
Full worked solution — 2023 S1 Q7(a)–(c)

(a) \(\mathbf v(t)=\mathbf r'(t)=16\pi\cos(\pi t)\,\mathbf i-8\pi\sin(2\pi t)\,\mathbf j\) and \(\mathbf a(t)=\mathbf v'(t)=-16\pi^2\sin(\pi t)\,\mathbf i-16\pi^2\cos(2\pi t)\,\mathbf j\).

(b) \(x=16\sin\pi t\Rightarrow\sin\pi t=\frac{x}{16}\). Using the hint with \(\theta=\pi t\):

$$y=4\cos2\pi t=4\big(1-2\sin^2\pi t\big)=4\Big(1-\frac{2x^2}{256}\Big)=4-\frac{x^2}{32}.$$

This is a downward parabola with vertex \((0,4)\). Since \(|x|\le16\), the hexbug oscillates along the arc from \((-16,-4)\) to \((16,-4)\).

(c) \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,4)\). We need \(16\sin\pi t=0\) and \(4\cos2\pi t=4\), i.e. \(\sin\pi t=0\) and \(\cos2\pi t=1\). Both first hold again at \(t=1\). (At \(t=1\) the bug is back at the vertex, now moving the other way.) So it first returns at \(\boxed{t=1}\).

A common wrong answer is \(t=2\), the period of \(\sin\pi t\). Returning to the start point only requires the position to repeat, not the velocity.

Sketch instances: 2025 S1 Q8(a) \((\cos t,\frac12\sin t)\), \(t\in[0,\pi]\): the upper half of the ellipse \(x^2+4y^2=1\) from \((1,0)\) to \((-1,0)\), anticlockwise. The marking scheme gives 1 mark for the equation and 2 for the sketch, which "must indicate start point and direction of travel". 2024 S1 Q8(b) \((t\cos t,t\sin t)\): spiral arc from the origin to \((0,\frac\pi2)\). 2022 S2 Q9(c) \((e^t\sin t,e^t\cos t)\): \(r=e^t\) grows from 1 to \(e^\pi\), from \((0,1)\) through \((e^{\pi/2},0)\) to \((0,-e^\pi)\), clockwise.

Type E — Direct work integral (non-conservative, or "use the formula") 📘 Workbook §5.4.3, 5.6~4–15 marks

Looks like: "Determine whether \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. Determine the work done … along [path]", where the answer to the first part is no. Or "Use the formula \(W=\int_a^b\mathbf F(t)\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\) to determine …", where they want the direct route even if a potential exists.

📄 Real exam question — 2021 S2 Q8 [15 marks]

Let \(\mathbf F(x,y)=-2y^2\,\mathbf i+3xy\,\mathbf j\).

  • (a) Determine whether \(\mathbf F\) is a conservative vector field.
  • (b) Determine the work done by the force \(\mathbf F\) in moving a particle along the path parametrised by \(\mathbf r(t)=(t^3-2t)\,\mathbf i+(t^2-2)\,\mathbf j,\ 0\le t\le1\).
Full worked solution — 2021 S2 Q8(a)–(b)

(a) \(P=-2y^2\Rightarrow P_y=-4y\) and \(Q=3xy\Rightarrow Q_x=3y\). Since \(-4y\ne3y\) (for \(y\ne0\)), \(\mathbf F\) is not conservative. So we must integrate directly.

(b) \(\mathbf r'(t)=(3t^2-2,\ 2t)\) and \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))=\big(-2(t^2-2)^2,\ 3(t^3-2t)(t^2-2)\big)\). Notice \(t^3-2t=t(t^2-2)\):

$$\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=-2(t^2-2)^2(3t^2-2)+6t^2(t^2-2)^2=(t^2-2)^2\big(-6t^2+4+6t^2\big)=4(t^2-2)^2.$$ $$W=\int_0^14(t^4-4t^2+4)\,dt=4\left(\tfrac15-\tfrac43+4\right)=4\cdot\tfrac{43}{15}=\boxed{\tfrac{172}{15}}.$$

Factor before you expand. Spotting \(t^3-2t=t(t^2-2)\) turns a degree-8 polynomial into \(4(t^2-2)^2\).

Other instances: 2020 S2 Q10: \(\mathbf F=(x,y,e^z\cos xy)\) on \(y=x^2,z=0\), \(x\) from \(-1\) to \(2\). With \(z=0\) the \(\mathbf k\)-component meets \(z'=0\), leaving \(\int_{-1}^2(t+2t^3)\,dt=9\). 2023 S1 Q8(b): helix \((\cos t,\sin t,t)\), \(\mathbf F=(-\frac x2,-\frac y2,\frac14)\). The integrand is \(\frac14\), so \(W=\frac{3\pi}4\), and part (d) checks this with \(f=-\frac{x^2+y^2}{4}+\frac z4\). 2025 S1 Q8(e): segment \((1-2t,0)\). \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=-2\), so \(W=-2\), the same as via the potential.

§5 · Wording triggers — the money table

Read the verb, then choose the tool. Most lost marks in this chapter come from computing the wrong thing, e.g. velocity when speed was asked, or a direct integral when path independence was the point.

A · What is being asked for?

If the question says…It means / do this
"velocity" / "velocity vector"\(\mathbf r'(t)\). A vector.
"speed"\(\|\mathbf r'(t)\|\). A scalar \(\ge0\).
"acceleration"\(\mathbf r''(t)\). A vector.
"position vector at time \(t\)" given \(\mathbf a\) or \(\mathbf v\)Integrate. Show the constants and fix them from the initial data.
"distance travelled" / "length of the path" / "arc length"\(\int_a^b\|\mathbf r'\|\,dt\). If the speed is constant, speed × time.
"how long before it hits the ground/water"Solve \(z(t)=0\) and take the positive root.
"maximum height"\(z'(t)=0\), then evaluate \(z\) there.
"first time …" / "first returns to its starting point"Smallest \(t\gt0\) solving the equation. Check that all coordinates match.
"velocity (tangent) parallel to the line …"\(\mathbf r'(t)=k\,\mathbf d\) with \(\mathbf d\) the line's direction. Cross-multiply to get \(\tan t=\dots\).

B · Parametrisation wording

If the question says…It means / do this
"line through \(P\) in the direction of \(\mathbf v\)"\(\mathbf r=P+t\mathbf v\), \(t\in\mathbb R\).
"straight line (path) from \(A\) to \(B\)"\(\mathbf r=A+t(B-A)\), \(0\le t\le1\). Order matters for work.
"clockwise" / "counterclockwise, starting from …"Choose signs so \(t=0\) is the start. Check the direction at \(t=\pi/2\).
"ellipse crossing the \(x\)-axis at \(\pm a\) and \(y\)-axis at \(\pm b\)"\((a\cos t,\ b\sin t)\), then adjust the start and direction.
"sketch the conic … find a parametric representation"Complete the square → standard form → \((h+a\cos t,\,k+b\sin t)\).
"show that it moves along a parabolic / elliptical path"Eliminate \(t\) (use the hint identity). State the equation and the portion traced.
"sketch the path; indicate the starting point and the direction of travel"Mark \(\mathbf r(a)\) and \(\mathbf r(b)\), and draw an arrow. Marks are lost without both.

C · Field / work wording

If the question says…It means / do this
"show that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative"State "conservative if \(P_y=Q_x\)", compute both, then say "equal ⇒ conservative". In 3-D check all three pairs.
"is \(\mathbf F\) conservative? explain / justify"Same test. The answer might be no, and then the work must be done directly.
"determine \(A\) and \(B\) such that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative"Write \(P_y\equiv Q_x\) and match coefficients of each monomial.
"find a potential function \(f\) such that \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\)" / "gradient field"Partial integration with \(+g(y)\). Check \(\nabla f=\mathbf F\).
"calculate the work … along [ugly \(\mathbf r(t)\)]" after a potential part\(W=f(\mathbf r(b))-f(\mathbf r(a))\). Only the endpoints are needed.
"use the potential function in part (c) to …"The fundamental theorem. Don't integrate.
"use the formula \(W=\int_a^b\mathbf F(t)\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\)"Direct route required, even if you know \(f\). Show Par / Diff / F in t / Dot / Int.
"use (c) to check your answer for (b)"Compute both ways and state that they agree.
"around the closed curve …"Conservative ⇒ \(W=0\), with the reason stated. Otherwise parametrise each piece and add.
"travels along the \(x\)-axis to …, then around the circle to …" (a composite path)Conservative: endpoints only. Otherwise one parametrisation per piece.

§6 · Traps & marks lost

Speed vs velocity. "Determine the speed" wants \(\|\mathbf r'\|\), a number or function. Writing the velocity vector gets at most the formula mark. The reverse also costs marks: 2024 S2 Q7(a) asks for velocity, and the marking scheme gives 3 of 4 marks "for correct velocity vector".
Forgetting the integration constants (or getting them from the wrong data). \(\mathbf v(0)\) fixes the constants in \(\mathbf v\), and \(\mathbf r(0)\) fixes those in \(\mathbf r\). A common slip is \(\mathbf r(t)=\mathbf v_0t-\frac12gt^2\) with the initial height (the 40 m cliff) missing.
Taking the negative root for the landing time. \(t^2=\frac{400}{49}\) has two roots. State "\(t\gt0\), so \(t=\frac{20}{7}\)".
\(\sqrt{A^2}=|A|\). For \(\mathbf r=(\cos^3t,\sin^3t)\), the speed is \(3|\sin t\cos t|\), not \(3\sin t\cos t\). Integrating the latter over \([0,2\pi]\) gives the absurd length 0. Split at the sign changes or use symmetry (4 × the first quadrant = 6).
Using the wrong orientation. Work changes sign when the path is reversed. "From \((1,0)\) to \((-1,0)\)" needs \(\mathbf r(0)=(1,0)\). The summary's claim that \(\omega\gt0\) is clockwise is wrong (see §1). Test with \(t=0\) and \(t=\pi/2\).
Doing the direct integral when the field is conservative. The 2025 S2 Q8 path \(x=t\cos\pi t,\ y=2t^2\sin\pi t\) is designed to be impossible by hand. If you have already shown \(\mathbf F\) conservative, the marks are for "\(W=f(\mathbf r(3))-f(\mathbf r(0))\)" plus correct endpoints (the 2024 S2 MS gives 3 marks for the endpoints and 3 for the evaluation).
Endpoint arithmetic. \(\cos3\pi=-1\), \(\sin3\pi=0\), \(\cos\frac\pi2=0\), \(e^{\pi}\cos\pi=-e^{\pi}\). Most finale errors are here, not in the calculus. Write \(\mathbf r(a)\) and \(\mathbf r(b)\) on their own line before substituting into \(f\).
The "\(+C\)" in a potential must be a function. When integrating \(f_x\) with respect to \(x\), write \(+g(y)\), not \(+C\). Otherwise you lose the pure-\(y\) terms (e.g. the \(-y^3\) in 2022 S2 Q9's \(f=3x+x^2y-y^3\), or the \(+y\) in 2024 S1 Q8).
Adding \(\int P\,dx\) and \(\int Q\,dy\). For \(\mathbf F=(2xy+1,\ x^2+1)\) this gives \(2x^2y+x+y\), whose gradient is \((4xy+1,\,2x^2+1)\ne\mathbf F\): the shared term is double-counted. Use \(g'(y)\) matching. The correct potential is \(x^2y+x+y\).
Stopping at "\(P_y=Q_x\)" with no conclusion. The statement of the criterion is worth marks (2 of 6 in 2024 S2 Q8(a)), and so is the concluding sentence. Write: "Since \(P_y=Q_x\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), \(\mathbf F\) is conservative."
Only one cross-partial checked in 3-D. A 3-D field needs \(P_y=Q_x\), \(P_z=R_x\) and \(Q_z=R_y\). Checking only the first proves nothing.
Sketches without start point or arrow. The 2025 S1 marking scheme says the sketch "must indicate start point and direction of travel". Also sketch only the portion traced: \(t\in[0,\pi]\) of \((\cos t,\frac12\sin t)\) is the upper half of the ellipse, not the full ellipse.
Eliminating \(t\) loses the domain. \((e^t,e^{2t})\) is only the right half of \(y=x^2\). \(16\sin\pi t\) confines the hexbug to \(|x|\le16\). State the restriction.
Official-solution typo to ignore. The 2022 S1 Q10 solution writes \(W=f(1,1)-f(0,1)\) but actually evaluates \(f(1,0)\), which is correct since the segment starts at \((1,0)\). The answer \(e^2\) is right. Always read the start point off the question.

§7 · Self-check

1. Parametrise the segment from \((2,-1)\) to \((5,3)\). How long is it?

\(\mathbf r(t)=(2,-1)+t(3,4)=(2+3t,\,-1+4t)\), \(0\le t\le1\). Its length is \(\|(3,4)\|=\boxed5\).

2. Does \((\cos t,\,\sin t)\) go clockwise or anticlockwise? How do you check without memorising?

Anticlockwise. At \(t=0\) it is at \((1,0)\), and at \(t=\pi/2\) it is at \((0,1)\): right → top is anticlockwise. (Or: \(\mathbf r'(0)=(0,1)\) points up at the rightmost point.)

3. A particle has \(\mathbf r'(t)=(3\cos t,\,-3\sin t,\,4)\). What is its speed? How far does it go for \(0\le t\le2\)?

Speed \(=\sqrt{9+16}=5\), constant, so the distance is \(5\times2=\boxed{10}\).

4. \(\mathbf a=(0,0,-10)\), \(\mathbf v(0)=(1,0,0)\), \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0,20)\). Write \(\mathbf r(t)\) and the landing time.

\(\mathbf r(t)=(t,\,0,\,20-5t^2)\). \(z=0\Rightarrow t^2=4\Rightarrow t=2\) (reject \(-2\)).

5. What is the difference between the arc-length integrand and the work integrand?

Arc length: \(\|\mathbf r'(t)\|\), which ignores direction and is always \(\ge0\). Work: \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\), which depends on the field and on the direction of travel, and can be negative.

6. State the test for \(\mathbf F=(P,Q)\) to be conservative, and the 3-D version.

2-D: \(P_y=Q_x\). 3-D: \(P_y=Q_x,\ P_z=R_x,\ Q_z=R_y\) (curl \(\mathbf F=\mathbf 0\)).

7. Is \(\mathbf F=(-y,\,x)\) conservative? What is the work once anticlockwise round the unit circle?

\(P_y=-1\ne1=Q_x\), so it is not conservative. With \(\mathbf r=(\cos t,\sin t)\): \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=\sin^2t+\cos^2t=1\), so \(W=2\pi\ne0\), consistent with non-conservative.

8. \(\mathbf F=\nabla(x^2y)\). Work from \((1,1)\) to \((2,3)\) along any path?

\(f(2,3)-f(1,1)=12-1=\boxed{11}\).

9. Find a potential for \(\mathbf F=(y+2x,\ x)\).

\(f=\int(y+2x)\,dx=xy+x^2+g(y)\), and \(f_y=x+g'=x\Rightarrow g'=0\). So \(f=xy+x^2\).

10. Why is the work of a conservative field around any closed loop zero?

\(W=f(\text{end})-f(\text{start})\), and on a closed loop the end equals the start, so \(W=0\).

11. You find \(g'(y)=2x\) while building a potential. What went wrong?

\(g'\) must depend on \(y\) only. Either there is an algebra slip, or \(\mathbf F\) is not conservative (re-check \(P_y=Q_x\)).

12. The speed of \((t^2\cos t,\,t^2\sin t,\,16-2t)\) is a perfect square. Of what?

\(\|\mathbf r'\|^2=t^4+4t^2+4=(t^2+2)^2\), so the speed is \(t^2+2\) (2024 S1 Q7).

13. The path is \(\mathbf r(t)=(t\cos t,\,t\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le\pi/2\). What are its endpoints?

\(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0)\) and \(\mathbf r(\pi/2)=(\frac\pi2\cos\frac\pi2,\ \frac\pi2\sin\frac\pi2)=(0,\frac\pi2)\).

14. How do you parametrise the upper half of \(x^2+4y^2=1\) from \((1,0)\) to \((-1,0)\)?

\((\cos t,\ \frac12\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le\pi\) (exactly 2025 S1 Q8's path).

(b) Replace \(t\) by the fraction of the journey completed, \(\frac{t-2}{5-2}\):

Q. Identify (with start point and direction) the paths (a) \(\mathbf r(t)=(1+2\cos t,\ -3+5\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\); (b) \(\mathbf r(t)=(e^t,\ e^{2t})\), \(t\in\mathbb R\).

(a) \(\cos t=\frac{x-1}{2}\) and \(\sin t=\frac{y+3}{5}\), so \(\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1\) gives

$$\frac{(x-1)^2}{4}+\frac{(y+3)^2}{25}=1,$$

an ellipse centred at \((1,-3)\) with semi-axes 2 (horizontal) and 5 (vertical). It starts at \(\mathbf r(0)=(3,-3)\), the rightmost point. \(\mathbf r'(0)=(0,5)\) points up, so the motion is anticlockwise, once around.

(b) \(y=e^{2t}=(e^t)^2=x^2\). But \(x=e^t\gt0\), so the path is only the right half of the parabola \(y=x^2\) (excluding the origin), traversed left to right as \(t\) increases.

EXAM-LEVEL · 8 marks Q4. Consider the conic \(9x^2+18x+y^2-4y+4=0\). (a) Complete the square and identify the curve. (b) Give a parametrisation. (c) Find all points on the curve where the velocity (tangent) vector is parallel to the line \(y=x\).

$$(x+1)^2+\frac{(y-2)^2}{9}=1:$$

an ellipse centred at \((-1,2)\) with semi-axes 1 (horizontal) and 3 (vertical).

(b) \(\mathbf r(t)=(-1+\cos t,\ 2+3\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\).

(c) \(\mathbf r'(t)=(-\sin t,\ 3\cos t)\). Parallel to \((1,1)\) requires \(-\sin t=3\cos t\), i.e. \(\tan t=-3\). Then \((\cos t,\sin t)=\pm\left(\tfrac1{\sqrt{10}},-\tfrac3{\sqrt{10}}\right)\), giving the two points

$$\Big(-1+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}},\ 2-\tfrac{9}{\sqrt{10}}\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad\Big(-1-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}},\ 2+\tfrac{9}{\sqrt{10}}\Big).$$

Check with implicit differentiation: \(\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{9(x+1)}{y-2}=-\frac{9/\sqrt{10}}{-9/\sqrt{10}}=1\) ✓.

STRETCH · 5 marks Q5. A bead moves with \(\mathbf r(t)=(\cos^2t,\ \sin^2t)\), \(0\le t\le\pi\). (a) Show that it stays on a straight line and describe exactly what it does. (b) Find the total distance it travels.

Solution

(a) \(x+y=\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1\), and \(0\le x\le1\). So the bead is on the segment from \((1,0)\) to \((0,1)\). It starts at \((1,0)\), reaches \((0,1)\) at \(t=\frac\pi2\), and comes back to \((1,0)\) at \(t=\pi\).

(b) \(\mathbf r'=(-2\sin t\cos t,\ 2\sin t\cos t)=(-\sin2t,\ \sin2t)\), so \(\|\mathbf r'\|=\sqrt2\,|\sin2t|\). By symmetry,

$$L=2\int_0^{\pi/2}\sqrt2\sin2t\,dt=2\sqrt2\Big[-\tfrac12\cos2t\Big]_0^{\pi/2}=2\sqrt2\cdot1=\boxed{2\sqrt2}.$$

That is twice the segment length \(\sqrt2\), as expected for there and back. (Integrating \(\sqrt2\sin2t\) over \([0,\pi]\) without the absolute value would give 0, which is wrong.)

Q. A particle has acceleration \(\mathbf a(t)=(6t,\,-2,\,e^t)\), initial velocity \(\mathbf v(0)=(1,4,0)\) and initial position \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0,1)\). Find \(\mathbf v(t)\) and \(\mathbf r(t)\), and the position when the \(y\)-component of velocity is zero.

$$\mathbf v(t)=(3t^2+1,\ 4-2t,\ e^t-1).$$

\(\mathbf r(t)=(t^3+t+c_4,\ 4t-t^2+c_5,\ e^t-t+c_6)\). From \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0,1)\): \(c_4=c_5=0,\ 1+c_6=1\Rightarrow c_6=0\). So

$$\mathbf r(t)=(t^3+t,\ 4t-t^2,\ e^t-t).$$

\(v_y=4-2t=0\Rightarrow t=2\), where \(\mathbf r(2)=(10,\ 4,\ e^2-2)\).

Q. A ball is kicked from the origin (ground level) with \(\mathbf v_0=(7,0,14.7)\) m/s under \(\mathbf a=(0,0,-9.8)\). Find the time of flight, the range and the maximum height.

EXAM-LEVEL · 8 marks Q4. A particle moves in the plane with \(\mathbf a(t)=(2,\,-6t)\), \(\mathbf v(0)=(-4,3)\), \(\mathbf r(0)=(1,0)\). (a) Find \(\mathbf r(t)\). (b) At what time \(t\gt0\) is it moving horizontally? Give its position and speed then.

STRETCH · 3 marks Q5. Prove that if a particle moves with constant speed, then its velocity and acceleration are perpendicular at every instant.

Solution

If \(\|\mathbf v\|=c\), then \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v=c^2\) for all \(t\). Differentiating with the product rule for dot products gives \(\mathbf v'\cdot\mathbf v+\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v'=2\,\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a=0\). So \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a=0\): the vectors are perpendicular (whenever both are nonzero). Q2(c) is an instance.

Q. Find the length of \(\mathbf r(t)=(3t^2,\ 2t^3)\), \(0\le t\le\sqrt3\).

Worked B — a graph \(y=g(x)\) with the "\(1+(A-B)^2=(A+B)^2\)" trick

Q. Find the length of \(y=\dfrac{x^3}{6}+\dfrac{1}{2x}\) for \(1\le x\le2\).

\(y'=\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac1{2x^2}\). With \(A=\frac{x^2}2\) and \(B=\frac1{2x^2}\), we have \(4AB=1\), so

$$1+y'^2=1+(A-B)^2=(A+B)^2=\Big(\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{1}{2x^2}\Big)^2.$$ $$L=\int_1^2\Big(\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{1}{2x^2}\Big)dx=\Big[\frac{x^3}{6}-\frac1{2x}\Big]_1^2=\Big(\frac43-\frac14\Big)-\Big(\frac16-\frac12\Big)=\boxed{\frac{17}{12}}.$$

✏️ Your turn

WARM-UP · 3 marks Q1. Find the length of the helix \(\mathbf r(t)=(5\cos t,\ 5\sin t,\ 12t)\) for \(0\le t\le\pi\).

Solution

\(\|\mathbf r'\|=\sqrt{25\sin^2t+25\cos^2t+144}=\sqrt{169}=13\), so \(L=13\pi\).

STANDARD · 5 marks Q2. Find the length of \(\mathbf r(t)=(2t,\ t^2,\ \ln t)\), \(1\le t\le e\).

Solution

\(\mathbf r'=(2,\ 2t,\ \tfrac1t)\), so \(\|\mathbf r'\|^2=4+4t^2+\frac1{t^2}=\left(2t+\frac1t\right)^2\) (the cross term is \(2\cdot2t\cdot\frac1t=4\)).

$$L=\int_1^e\Big(2t+\frac1t\Big)dt=\big[t^2+\ln t\big]_1^e=(e^2+1)-(1+0)=\boxed{e^2}.$$

EXAM-LEVEL · 6 marks Q3. Consider the helix \(\mathbf r(t)=(3\cos2t,\ 3\sin2t,\ 8t)\), \(0\le t\le a\). Find \(a\) such that the helix has length 30.

Solution

\(\mathbf r'=(-6\sin2t,\ 6\cos2t,\ 8)\), so \(\|\mathbf r'\|=\sqrt{36+64}=10\). Then \(L=\int_0^a10\,dt=10a=30\), giving \(a=3\).

EXAM-LEVEL · 7 marks Q4. The astroid is \(\mathbf r(t)=(\cos^3t,\ \sin^3t)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\). (a) Show that \(\|\mathbf r'(t)\|=3|\sin t\cos t|\). (b) Find the total length of the astroid.

Solution

(a) \(\mathbf r'=(-3\cos^2t\sin t,\ 3\sin^2t\cos t)\), so \(\|\mathbf r'\|^2=9\sin^2t\cos^2t(\cos^2t+\sin^2t)=9\sin^2t\cos^2t\), and \(\|\mathbf r'\|=3|\sin t\cos t|\).

(b) By symmetry the length is 4 times the first-quadrant arc, where \(\sin t\cos t\ge0\):

$$L=4\int_0^{\pi/2}3\sin t\cos t\,dt=4\cdot\Big[\tfrac32\sin^2t\Big]_0^{\pi/2}=4\cdot\tfrac32=\boxed6.$$

\(\int_0^{2\pi}3\sin t\cos t\,dt=0\). Dropping the absolute value gives a "length" of zero.

STRETCH · 5 marks Q5. Find the length of the curve \(y=\dfrac{x^2}{4}-\dfrac{\ln x}{2}\), \(1\le x\le e\).

Solution

\(y'=\frac x2-\frac1{2x}\). Here \(4\cdot\frac x2\cdot\frac1{2x}=1\), so \(1+y'^2=\left(\frac x2+\frac1{2x}\right)^2\).

$$L=\int_1^e\Big(\frac x2+\frac1{2x}\Big)dx=\Big[\frac{x^2}{4}+\frac{\ln x}{2}\Big]_1^e=\frac{e^2}4+\frac12-\frac14=\boxed{\frac{e^2+1}{4}}.$$

8.4 · Work by direct computation \(\int\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\)📘 Workbook §5.4.3, 5.6

📖 Learn from these

Worked A — same endpoints, two paths, two answers

Q. \(\mathbf F=(y^2,\ 2x)\). Find the work from \((0,0)\) to \((1,2)\) along (a) the segment \(y=2x\); (b) the parabola \(y=2x^2\).

(a) \(\mathbf r=(t,2t)\), \(\mathbf r'=(1,2)\), \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r)=(4t^2,\ 2t)\). The integrand is \(4t^2+4t\), so \(W=\frac43+2=\boxed{\frac{10}3}\).

(b) \(\mathbf r=(t,2t^2)\), \(\mathbf r'=(1,4t)\), \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r)=(4t^4,\ 2t)\). The integrand is \(4t^4+8t^2\), so \(W=\frac45+\frac83=\boxed{\frac{52}{15}}\).

The answers differ, so the work is path-dependent. Indeed \(P_y=2y\ne2=Q_x\).

Worked B — a 3-D field along a helix (integration by parts)

Q. \(\mathbf F=(z,\ x,\ y)\) along \(\mathbf r(t)=(\cos t,\sin t,t)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\).

\(\mathbf F(\mathbf r)=(t,\ \cos t,\ \sin t)\) and \(\mathbf r'=(-\sin t,\ \cos t,\ 1)\), so \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=-t\sin t+\cos^2t+\sin t\).

By parts, \(\int_0^{2\pi}-t\sin t\,dt=\big[t\cos t\big]_0^{2\pi}-\int_0^{2\pi}\cos t\,dt=2\pi-0=2\pi\). Also \(\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2t\,dt=\pi\) and \(\int_0^{2\pi}\sin t\,dt=0\). So \(W=\boxed{3\pi}\).

WARM-UP · 3 marks Q1. A constant force \(\mathbf F=(3,-1)\) moves a particle along the straight line from \((1,1)\) to \((4,5)\). Find the work (a) by \(W=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf d\), (b) using a parametrisation.

Solution

(a) \(\mathbf d=(3,4)\), so \(W=9-4=5\).

(b) \(\mathbf r=(1+3t,\ 1+4t)\) and \(\mathbf r'=(3,4)\), so \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=5\) and \(W=\int_0^15\,dt=5\) ✓.

STANDARD · 6 marks Q2. Find the work done by \(\mathbf F=(-y,\ x^2)\) along the quarter-circle of radius 2 from \((2,0)\) to \((0,2)\), anticlockwise.

Solution

\(\mathbf r=(2\cos t,2\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le\frac\pi2\), with \(\mathbf r'=(-2\sin t,2\cos t)\) and \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r)=(-2\sin t,\ 4\cos^2t)\).

$$\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=4\sin^2t+8\cos^3t.$$

\(\int_0^{\pi/2}4\sin^2t\,dt=4\cdot\frac\pi4=\pi\). For the cube, \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^3t\,dt=\int_0^{\pi/2}(1-\sin^2t)\cos t\,dt=\big[\sin t-\tfrac{\sin^3t}3\big]_0^{\pi/2}=\frac23\). So

$$W=\pi+8\cdot\tfrac23=\boxed{\pi+\tfrac{16}{3}}.$$

STANDARD · 5 marks Q3. Find the work done by \(\mathbf F(x,y,z)=(2y,\ z,\ x)\) along the twisted cubic \(\mathbf r(t)=(t,\ t^2,\ t^3)\), \(0\le t\le1\).

Solution

\(\mathbf F(\mathbf r)=(2t^2,\ t^3,\ t)\) and \(\mathbf r'=(1,\ 2t,\ 3t^2)\), so \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=2t^2+2t^4+3t^3\).

$$W=\tfrac23+\tfrac25+\tfrac34=\tfrac{40+24+45}{60}=\boxed{\tfrac{109}{60}}.$$

EXAM-LEVEL · 8 marks Q4. Let \(\mathbf F=(y^2,\,-x)\). (a) Determine whether \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. (b) Find the work done along \(\mathbf r(t)=(t^2,\ t^3)\), \(0\le t\le1\). (c) Compare with the work along the straight segment from \((0,0)\) to \((1,1)\).

Solution

(a) \(P_y=2y\) and \(Q_x=-1\). These are not equal, so \(\mathbf F\) is not conservative.

(b) \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r)=(t^6,\ -t^2)\) and \(\mathbf r'=(2t,\ 3t^2)\), so the integrand is \(2t^7-3t^4\) and \(W=\frac14-\frac35=\boxed{-\frac{7}{20}}\).

(c) \(\mathbf r=(t,t)\): the integrand is \(t^2-t\), so \(W=\frac13-\frac12=-\frac16\ne-\frac7{20}\). The work differs between paths, consistent with (a).

STRETCH · 7 marks Q5. \(\mathbf F=(x+y,\ y-x)\). Compute the work done once around the triangle \((0,0)\to(1,0)\to(0,1)\to(0,0)\). What does the answer tell you?

Solution

Three legs, each on \(0\le t\le1\):

  • \(C_1\): \(\mathbf r=(t,0)\), \(\mathbf r'=(1,0)\), \(\mathbf F=(t,-t)\). Integrand \(t\), so \(W_1=\frac12\).
  • \(C_2\): \(\mathbf r=(1-t,t)\), \(\mathbf r'=(-1,1)\), \(\mathbf F=(1,\ 2t-1)\). Integrand \(-1+2t-1=2t-2\), so \(W_2=1-2=-1\).
  • \(C_3\): \(\mathbf r=(0,1-t)\), \(\mathbf r'=(0,-1)\), \(\mathbf F=(1-t,\ 1-t)\). Integrand \(-(1-t)\), so \(W_3=-\frac12\).

Total \(W=\frac12-1-\frac12=\boxed{-1}\). The loop is closed but \(W\ne0\), so \(\mathbf F\) is not conservative (check: \(P_y=1\ne-1=Q_x\)).

8.5 · Conservative test & constructing potentials (2-D and 3-D)📘 Workbook §5.5

📖 Learn from these

Worked A — 2-D potential with a nonzero \(g(y)\)

Q. Show \(\mathbf F=(2xe^y+y,\ x^2e^y+x-2y)\) is conservative and find a potential.

\(P_y=2xe^y+1\) and \(Q_x=2xe^y+1\). These are equal on \(\mathbb R^2\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.

\(f=\int(2xe^y+y)\,dx=x^2e^y+xy+g(y)\). Then \(f_y=x^2e^y+x+g'(y)\). Setting this equal to \(x^2e^y+x-2y\) gives \(g'(y)=-2y\), so \(g=-y^2\).

\(\boxed{f=x^2e^y+xy-y^2}\). Check: \(\nabla f=(2xe^y+y,\ x^2e^y+x-2y)\) ✓.

Worked B — a 3-D potential in three rounds

Q. Show \(\mathbf F=(2xz+y,\ x+z^2,\ x^2+2yz)\) is conservative and find a potential.

Test: \(P_y=1=Q_x\), \(P_z=2x=R_x\), \(Q_z=2z=R_y\). All three pairs agree, so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.

Round 1: \(f=\int(2xz+y)\,dx=x^2z+xy+g(y,z)\).
Round 2: \(f_y=x+g_y=x+z^2\Rightarrow g_y=z^2\Rightarrow g=yz^2+h(z)\).
Round 3: \(f_z=x^2+2yz+h'(z)=x^2+2yz\Rightarrow h'=0\).

\(\boxed{f=x^2z+xy+yz^2}\).

✏️ Your turn

WARM-UP · 4 marks Q1. Decide whether each field is conservative. If so, find a potential. (a) \(\mathbf F=(4x^3+y,\ x+\cos y)\). (b) \(\mathbf G=(xy,\ xy)\).

Solution

(a) \(P_y=1=Q_x\), so it is conservative. \(f=x^4+xy+g(y)\), and \(f_y=x+g'=x+\cos y\Rightarrow g=\sin y\). So \(f=x^4+xy+\sin y\).

(b) \(P_y=x\) and \(Q_x=y\). These are not equal (e.g. at \((1,0)\)), so \(\mathbf G\) is not conservative.

STANDARD · 5 marks Q2. On the half-plane \(y\gt0\), let \(\mathbf F=\left(\ln y+2x,\ \dfrac{x}{y}+3y^2\right)\). Show \(\mathbf F\) is conservative and find a potential.

Solution

\(P_y=\frac1y\) and \(Q_x=\frac1y\). They are equal on \(y\gt0\), a region with no holes, so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative there.

\(f=\int(\ln y+2x)\,dx=x\ln y+x^2+g(y)\). Then \(f_y=\frac xy+g'(y)=\frac xy+3y^2\Rightarrow g=y^3\). So \(\boxed{f=x\ln y+x^2+y^3}\).

EXAM-LEVEL · 12 marks Q3. Let \(\mathbf F=(6xy^2+Ay^3)\,\mathbf i+(Bx^2y+9xy^2+4y)\,\mathbf j\).

  • (a) (5 marks) Determine the values of \(A\) and \(B\) such that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.
  • (b) (5 marks) Using these values, find a potential \(f\) with \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\).
  • (c) (2 marks) Find the work done by \(\mathbf F\) along any path from \((0,0)\) to \((1,1)\).
Solution

(a) \(\mathbf F\) is conservative if \(P_y=Q_x\). \(P_y=12xy+3Ay^2\) and \(Q_x=2Bxy+9y^2\). For these to agree for all \(x,y\): \(xy\)-terms give \(12=2B\Rightarrow B=6\), and \(y^2\)-terms give \(3A=9\Rightarrow A=3\).

(b) \(f=\int(6xy^2+3y^3)\,dx=3x^2y^2+3xy^3+g(y)\). Then \(f_y=6x^2y+9xy^2+g'(y)=6x^2y+9xy^2+4y\Rightarrow g=2y^2\). So \(\boxed{f=3x^2y^2+3xy^3+2y^2}\).

(c) \(W=f(1,1)-f(0,0)=3+3+2=\boxed8\).

EXAM-LEVEL · 8 marks Q4. Let \(\mathbf F(x,y,z)=(yz,\ xz+\cos y,\ xy+2z)\). (a) Show \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. (b) Find a potential. (c) Find the work done moving from \((0,0,0)\) to \((1,\frac\pi2,2)\).

Solution

(a) \(P_y=z=Q_x\), \(P_z=y=R_x\), \(Q_z=x=R_y\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative on \(\mathbb R^3\).

(b) \(f=xyz+g(y,z)\). Then \(f_y=xz+g_y=xz+\cos y\Rightarrow g=\sin y+h(z)\), and \(f_z=xy+h'(z)=xy+2z\Rightarrow h=z^2\). So \(\boxed{f=xyz+\sin y+z^2}\).

(c) \(W=f(1,\tfrac\pi2,2)-f(0,0,0)=\pi+1+4=\boxed{\pi+5}\).

STRETCH · 3 marks Q5. For \(\mathbf F=(2xy+1,\ x^2+1)\), a student computes \(\int P\,dx+\int Q\,dy=(x^2y+x)+(x^2y+y)\) and claims \(f=2x^2y+x+y\). Show this is wrong and give the correct potential.

Solution

\(\nabla(2x^2y+x+y)=(4xy+1,\ 2x^2+1)\ne\mathbf F\): the shared term \(x^2y\) has been counted twice. The correct method is \(f=x^2y+x+g(y)\), then \(f_y=x^2+g'=x^2+1\Rightarrow g=y\). So \(\boxed{f=x^2y+x+y}\).

8.6 · Fundamental theorem, path independence & closed loops (the 18-mark finale)📘 Workbook §5.5

📖 Learn from these

Worked A — full finale on an unintegrable path

Q. Let \(\mathbf F(x,y)=(2xy^3+\cos x,\ 3x^2y^2+1)\). (a) (5) Show \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. (b) (7) Find a potential. (c) (6) Find the work along \(x(t)=\frac{\pi t}{2},\ y(t)=t^3+\sin(\pi t)\), \(0\le t\le1\).

(a) With \(P=2xy^3+\cos x\) and \(Q=3x^2y^2+1\), \(\mathbf F\) is conservative if \(P_y=Q_x\). \(P_y=6xy^2\) and \(Q_x=6xy^2\). They are equal on \(\mathbb R^2\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.

(b) \(f=\int(2xy^3+\cos x)\,dx=x^2y^3+\sin x+g(y)\). Then \(f_y=3x^2y^2+g'(y)=3x^2y^2+1\Rightarrow g'(y)=1\Rightarrow g(y)=y\). So \(\boxed{f=x^2y^3+\sin x+y}\). Check \(\nabla f=\mathbf F\) ✓.

(c) Since \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\), \(W=f(\mathbf r(1))-f(\mathbf r(0))\). The endpoints are \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,\,0+\sin0)=(0,0)\) and \(\mathbf r(1)=(\frac\pi2,\,1+\sin\pi)=(\frac\pi2,1)\).

$$W=\Big(\frac{\pi^2}{4}\cdot1+\sin\frac\pi2+1\Big)-0=\boxed{\frac{\pi^2}{4}+2}.$$

The numerical direct integral also gives \(4.4674\ldots=\frac{\pi^2}4+2\) (SymPy-verified), but by hand it is hopeless. That is the point of the question.

Worked B — a closed loop, both ways

Q. \(\mathbf F=(2x+y,\ x+2y)\). Find the work once around the ellipse \(\mathbf r(t)=(3\cos t,\,2\sin t)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\).

Via the potential. \(P_y=1=Q_x\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative with \(f=x^2+xy+y^2\). The curve is closed (\(\mathbf r(0)=\mathbf r(2\pi)=(3,0)\)), so \(W=f(3,0)-f(3,0)=\boxed0\).

Directly (check). \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=(6\cos t+2\sin t)(-3\sin t)+(3\cos t+4\sin t)(2\cos t)=-10\sin t\cos t+6\cos^2t-6\sin^2t\). Each term integrates to 0 over \([0,2\pi]\), so \(W=0\) ✓.

WARM-UP · 2 marks Q1. \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\) with \(f(x,y)=x^2y\). Find the work done by \(\mathbf F\) along any path from \((1,2)\) to \((3,-1)\).

Solution

\(W=f(3,-1)-f(1,2)=-9-2=\boxed{-11}\).

STANDARD · 8 marks Q2. Let \(\mathbf F=(y^2+2x,\ 2xy+3)\) and \(C:\ \mathbf r(t)=\big(t\cos(\pi t),\ t^2+t\sin(\pi t)\big)\), \(0\le t\le2\). Find the work done by \(\mathbf F\) along \(C\).

Solution

\(P_y=2y=Q_x\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. \(f=xy^2+x^2+g(y)\), and \(f_y=2xy+g'=2xy+3\Rightarrow g=3y\), giving \(f=xy^2+x^2+3y\).

Endpoints: \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0)\) and \(\mathbf r(2)=(2\cos2\pi,\ 4+2\sin2\pi)=(2,4)\).

\(W=f(2,4)-f(0,0)=32+4+12=\boxed{48}\). (The direct integral agrees, verified.)

EXAM-LEVEL · 18 marks Q3. Consider the force field \(\mathbf F(x,y)=(e^y-y\sin x,\ xe^y+\cos x)\).

  • (a) (5 marks) Show that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.
  • (b) (7 marks) Determine a potential function \(f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) such that \(\mathbf F=\nabla f\).
  • (c) (4 marks) Calculate the work done by \(\mathbf F\) moving a particle along \(x(t)=\pi t^2,\ y(t)=t^3+t\sin(\pi t)\), \(0\le t\le1\).
  • (d) (2 marks) The particle then returns to the origin along the straight line. What is the total work for the round trip?
Solution

(a) \(P=e^y-y\sin x\Rightarrow P_y=e^y-\sin x\). \(Q=xe^y+\cos x\Rightarrow Q_x=e^y-\sin x\). These are equal on \(\mathbb R^2\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative.

(b) \(f=\int(e^y-y\sin x)\,dx=xe^y+y\cos x+g(y)\). Then \(f_y=xe^y+\cos x+g'(y)=xe^y+\cos x\Rightarrow g'=0\). So \(\boxed{f=xe^y+y\cos x}\).

(c) \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0)\) and \(\mathbf r(1)=(\pi,\ 1+\sin\pi)=(\pi,1)\). So \(W=f(\pi,1)-f(0,0)=\pi e+\cos\pi-0=\boxed{\pi e-1}\).

(d) The round trip is a closed curve and \(\mathbf F\) is conservative, so the total work is \(\boxed0\). (The return leg alone contributes \(1-\pi e\).)

EXAM-LEVEL · 10 marks Q4. Let \(\mathbf F=(y\cos x,\ \sin x+2y)\). (a) Find a potential and use it to compute the work along \(C:\ \mathbf r(t)=\big(\frac\pi2\sin\frac{\pi t}{2},\ 2t^2\big)\), \(0\le t\le1\). (b) Let \(C_1\) be the straight segment from \((0,0)\) to \((\frac\pi2,2)\). Parametrise \(C_1\) and use \(W=\int_a^b\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\) to compute the work along \(C_1\) directly. Comment.

Solution

(a) \(P_y=\cos x=Q_x\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. \(f=y\sin x+g(y)\), and \(f_y=\sin x+g'=\sin x+2y\Rightarrow g=y^2\), so \(f=y\sin x+y^2\). \(C\) runs from \(\mathbf r(0)=(0,0)\) to \(\mathbf r(1)=(\frac\pi2,2)\), so \(W=f(\frac\pi2,2)-f(0,0)=2+4=\boxed6\).

(b) \(\mathbf r(t)=(\frac\pi2t,\ 2t)\), \(0\le t\le1\), with \(\mathbf r'=(\frac\pi2,2)\) and \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r)=(2t\cos\frac{\pi t}2,\ \sin\frac{\pi t}2+4t)\).

$$\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=\pi t\cos\tfrac{\pi t}{2}+2\sin\tfrac{\pi t}{2}+8t.$$

By parts: \(\int_0^1\pi t\cos\frac{\pi t}2\,dt=\pi\Big[\frac{2t}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi t}2+\frac4{\pi^2}\cos\frac{\pi t}2\Big]_0^1=\pi\Big(\frac2\pi-\frac4{\pi^2}\Big)=2-\frac4\pi\). Next, \(\int_0^12\sin\frac{\pi t}2\,dt=\frac4\pi\) and \(\int_0^18t\,dt=4\).

\(W=2-\frac4\pi+\frac4\pi+4=\boxed6\). This is the same as along \(C\), as path independence predicts.

STRETCH · 5 marks Q5. A student claims \(\mathbf F=(x-y,\ x+y)\) is conservative "because it's a nice linear field". Disprove the claim in two independent ways.

Solution

Test: \(P_y=-1\ne1=Q_x\).

Closed loop: once anticlockwise around \(x^2+y^2=9\) with \(\mathbf r=(3\cos t,3\sin t)\): \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=(3\cos t-3\sin t)(-3\sin t)+(3\cos t+3\sin t)(3\cos t)=9\). So \(W=\int_0^{2\pi}9\,dt=18\pi\ne0\). A conservative field would give 0.
