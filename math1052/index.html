⚙️ The Master Decision Engine

The exam never says "use the two-path test" or "this is a conservative field, use the potential." It gives you a function and asks a question. The key skill is translating wording → routine. Use the trigger table to decode a phrase, then the decision tree to pick the exact multivariable procedure. Each chapter's §5 has the full trigger table; this is the whole-course summary.

Read the verb first. "Determine / find / evaluate" = compute it. "Show that" = the answer is given, and every step of the working earns the marks. "Justify / explain / does it exist?" = a conclusion sentence that carries its own mark. "Sketch" = marks for labels (intercepts, asymptotes, levels, start point and arrow), not for artistry.

B. Decision tree: which multivariable procedure?

Walk it top-down. The order follows the course (and roughly the paper): limits → partials → approximation → gradient → chain/implicit → critical points → Lagrange → curves → line integrals.

① Is there a limit or "continuous at \((0,0)\)"?
Same weighted degree top & bottom e.g. \(\frac{x^2y}{x^4+y^2}\) → expect DNE.
Two-path test: axes / \(y=mx\) first; if they agree, the curve that balances degrees (\(y=x^2\), \(y=x^3\), \(x=t^5\)-type). Two values + sentence. 2023 S2 Q8, 2024 S1 Q2, 2025 S1 Q2.
Top degree higher / only \(x^2+y^2\) / a bounded factor → expect the limit exists.
Polar (\(\theta\) must vanish) or squeeze \(|\ldots|\le Cr\to0\). Then continuity: compare with \(f(0,0)\); "find \(c\)" ⇒ \(c=\) limit. 2021 S2 Q3, 2024 S2 Q2(b).
"Do the partials exist at (0,0)?"
Definition: \(f_x(0,0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(h,0)-f(0,0)}{h}\). Classic twist: discontinuous yet partials exist. 2025 S1 Q2, 2025 S2 Q4.
② Partials, tangent plane or an estimate?
Surface \(z=f(x,y)\)
Tangent plane \(z=f+f_x(x-a)+f_y(y-b)\) · normal \(\langle f_x,f_y,-1\rangle\). 2025 S2 Q2.
Level surface \(F=k\)
Normal \(=\nabla F(P)\) (→ ④ territory). 2021 S2 Q2.
"Estimate / approximate"
Linear \(P(x,y)\) (sheet), or quadratic \(Q(x,y)\) if asked; errors ⇒ \(|f_x|E_1+|f_y|E_2\). 2022 S1 Q1, 2022 S2 Q1, 2021 S2 Q9.
③ Rates in a direction?
Given \(f\), a point and a direction
\(\nabla f(P)\) → \(D_{\mathbf u}f=\nabla f\cdot\mathbf v/\|\mathbf v\|\). Max increase: \(\nabla f\), rate \(\|\nabla f\|\). No change: \(\perp\nabla f\). 2023 S1 Q1, 2024 S1 Q1, 2024 S2 Q4.
Given the rates, find \(\nabla f\)
Reverse problem: set \(\nabla f=(a,b)\), one equation per rate, solve. 2025 S1 Q1, 2023 S2 Q7, 2022 S1 Q6.
④ Variables depend on other variables?
\(z=f(x,y)\), \(x,y\) functions of \(t\) or \((s,t)\)
Chain rule + tree diagram; related rates with signs; "in terms of \(u,v\)" means substitute back. 2023 S1 Q4, 2024 S1 Q4, 2025 S1 Q4, 2024 S2 Q6.
\(g=f(\text{combination})\), "show that"
Name the inside \(u\), \(g_x=f'(u)u_x\), \(g_y=f'(u)u_y\), combine to the target. 2025 S2 Q3, 2022 S1 Q4.
A curve \(F(x,y)=0\)
Implicit \(y'=-F_x/F_y\) → tangent line. 2024 S2 Q3, 2022 S1 Q5, 2021 S1 Q3.
⑤ Max / min?
No constraint "critical points / classify"
Hessian test: \(\nabla f=\mathbf 0\) by cases → \(D\) table. \(D=0\) ⇒ slice argument. "Global max?" ⇒ a line with \(f\to\pm\infty\). 2025 S2 Q6, 2023 S1 Q5, 2023 S2 Q1.
Closed bounded region triangle / square / disk
EVT: interior critical points + each edge (1-D, endpoints) + corners → compare. 2024 S1 Q5, 2025 S1 Q5.
On a curve / surface "subject to", "on the sphere"
Lagrange: \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\) + \(g=c\); factor, never divide; evaluate all candidates; justify existence (closed & bounded, or growth). on every 2021–25 paper.
⑥ A moving point \(\mathbf r(t)\)?
Given \(\mathbf a\) or \(\mathbf v\)
Integrate \(\mathbf a\to\mathbf v\to\mathbf r\), one constant per component; then events (\(z=0\), max height, first time). 2025 S1 Q7, 2025 S2 Q7, 2023 S1 Q7.
Given \(\mathbf r(t)\)
Speed \(\|\mathbf r'\|\) (simplifies by design) → arc length \(\int\|\mathbf r'\|\,dt\). 2024 S1 Q7, 2024 S2 Q7, 2023 S2 Q5.
⑦ Work / line integral of \(\mathbf F\)?
\(P_y=Q_x\) holds conservative
Potential \(f\) → \(W=f(\text{end})-f(\text{start})\). Closed loop ⇒ 0. The path can be as ugly as it likes. Q8 on 5 of the 6 recent papers.
\(P_y\ne Q_x\) or "use the formula"
Direct: \(\int_a^b\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\), piece by piece. 2021 S2 Q8, 2020 S2 Q10.
Two forks that lose the most marks. (1) Limit exists vs DNE: paths can only ever prove non-existence. Agreement along ten lines proves nothing. Existence needs polar (with \(\theta\) gone) or a squeeze. (2) Lagrange algebra: dividing by \(x\), \(z\) or \(\lambda\) throws away a case, and in 2022 S1 and 2023 S1 that lost case held the answer. Factor, then branch.
For contours (Ch 3) and ODEs (Ch 1–2) there's no tree needed. Contours are always "complete the square → standard form → label". ODEs are "classify the equation (separable / linear / constant-coefficient 2nd-order) → its one recipe". See each chapter's §2.

⏱️ Exam-day strategy

The 10-minute planning playbook

You get 10 minutes of planning time before writing starts. Use it to sort the questions, not to admire them.

  1. Label all 8 questions by routine using the slot map below (e.g. "Q2 limit, two-path", "Q6 Lagrange, sphere", "Q8 conservative → potential"). In the S1 papers the order has been identical three years running.
  2. Mark each part ✓ (instant), ~ (routine but long), or ? (needs an idea). This gives you your running order.
  3. Find the idea for each "?" part now: which path kills the denominator, which substitution for the chain rule, whether \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. You can often answer these in your head.
  4. Check what the formula sheet gives (approximations, \(D_{\mathbf u}f\), chain rule, implicit, Hessian, Lagrange, work, arc length) and what it does not give: limits, continuity, the conservative test, potentials, the \(D\) classification rules, every ODE method. Full list.
  5. Start with the fastest full question: usually the gradient question (Q1 in S1 papers), the Q7 motion question or Q8 conservative field. Bank ~40 marks of pure routine early.

Time per mark

100 marks in 120 minutes = 1.2 min per mark. Work at ~1.1 min/mark and bank ~10 minutes for checking. Questions are 10, 12, 13, 14 or 18 marks:

2025 S2 at target pace: 6 × 13 + 11 + 20 = 109 min, leaving 11 min to check.

When to skip and come back

  • Hard stop reached: write the method line (formula, set-up, the system you'd solve) and move on. Method marks are paid per step. A correct set-up with a wrong final answer still earns most of the marks.
  • Skip the known time sinks until last: the curved-path limit when lines all agree (2023 S2 Q8b), a sketch with many labels, a Lagrange system with three or more cases, and the edges of a closed region.
  • Never skip a "show that" entirely. The answer is given, so later parts can use it even if you can't prove it. Write "using the given result" and carry on.
  • Later parts often don't need earlier ones. A potential function makes the work part a one-line subtraction. "Does \(f\) attain a global max?" needs only a line along which \(f\to\infty\).
  • Last 10 minutes: check signs (\(x-a\) with negative base points, \(\cos 3\pi\), end minus start), that each constraint holds at your Lagrange candidates, and that every direction vector was normalised.

The write-up rituals (from the Notebook)

Critical points: 5 lines
  1. \(f_x=\ldots,\ f_y=\ldots\)
  2. Solve \(f_x=f_y=0\) by cases; list every point
  3. \(f_{xx},f_{xy},f_{yy}\) and \(D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2\) as a formula
  4. Table: point · \(D\) · sign of \(f_{xx}\) · type
  5. Conclusion sentence per point (\(D=0\) ⇒ "inconclusive" + slice)
→ worked instance
Lagrange: 4 lines
  1. "Maximise \(f=\ldots\) subject to \(g=c\). By Lagrange, \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\)."
  2. The full system, including the constraint
  3. Solve by cases (factor, never divide); list all candidates
  4. Evaluate \(f\) at every candidate + why extremes exist (EVT or growth)
→ worked instance
Conservative → potential → work: 3 lines
  1. "\(P_y=\ldots=Q_x\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), so \(\mathbf F\) is conservative."
  2. \(f=\int P\,dx+h(y)\); \(f_y=Q\) fixes \(h\); check \(\nabla f=\mathbf F\)
  3. "By the fundamental theorem, \(W=f(\mathbf r(b))-f(\mathbf r(a))=\ldots\)"
→ worked instance
Continuity / limits
  1. Write the definition: "continuous at \((0,0)\) iff \(\lim f=f(0,0)\)"
  2. DNE: path 1 → value, path 2 → different value
  3. Exists: polar/squeeze with the bound shown
  4. Conclusion sentence ("so \(f\) is not continuous at …")
→ worked instance

How to present a sketch

  • Show the algebra that gives the shape: complete the square, then write the standard form ("\(=1\)"). In the 2024 S2 scheme a contour sketch was 1 (level value) + 3 (working to standard form) + 2 (intercepts) + 2 (graph).
  • Name the curve: "ellipse centred \((1,-2)\), semi-axes 3 and 2" or "hyperbola opening left/right".
  • Label every intercept with exact coordinates (surds, not decimals). Write "no \(y\)-intercepts" if there are none.
  • Asymptotes dashed, with their equations. Level value written on each contour.
  • Domain restrictions: \(z=\sqrt{\ldots}\) gives half-curves; state them before squaring (2025 S2 Q1).
  • Paths: start point, end point, an arrow for the direction of travel, and the portion traced (e.g. \((e^t,e^{2t})\) is only the right half of \(y=x^2\)).
  • Use a ruler for axes, keep the sketch big (half a page), and don't spend time on shading.

No calculator: exact values only

Keep \(\sqrt2\), \(\ln 2\), \(e^6\), \(\pi\), \(\tfrac{0.06}{7}\) exact until the last line. Questions are designed with clean numbers. If you get \(\sqrt{47.3}\), you probably made an arithmetic slip, so go back one step. For a numerical estimate (linear approximation), the paper gives the hint value you need (e.g. \(\ln 2\approx0.6931\)). Drill the trig table and standard integrals on Foundations.

Correction to the "obvious" slot map

A plausible guess is Q1 contours · Q2 partials/tangent plane · Q3 gradient/chain rule · Q4 limits · Q5 Lagrange · Q6 critical points. That is exactly 2025 S2 and no other paper. The three S1 papers instead run gradient → limits → contours → chain rule → critical points/closed region → Lagrange. If your exam is a Semester 2 paper, expect the same eight topics in a different order. Plan in the first minute and don't rely on the order.

Things the papers keep doing

  • The conservative-field finale is the biggest single question. 14–18 marks, with an ugly path you never parametrise. The same spiral \((t\cos t,\,t\sin t)\), \(t\in[0,\pi/2]\) appeared in both 2024 papers' Q8.
  • Discontinuous but the partials exist. 2025 S1 Q2 and 2025 S2 Q4 both used this twist. Know the \(h\)-limit definition cold.
  • Completing the square does triple duty. It gives the contour shape, the intercepts and the extreme value (2024 S1 Q3 min \(z=1\); 2024 S2 Q1 max \(T=100\)).
  • The Lagrange case you'd cancel is the answer. \(x=0\) in 2022 S1, \(\lambda=0\) in 2023 S1, \(z=0\) cases on the sphere. Factor, never divide.
  • Reverse problems. "Here are the rates, find \(\nabla f\)" (2025 S1 Q1, 2023 S2 Q7, 2022 S1 Q6). This is the same dot product, solved backwards.
  • Speed simplifies by design. If \(\|\mathbf r'\|\) doesn't collapse to a perfect square or a constant, recheck your derivative.
