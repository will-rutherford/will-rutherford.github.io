Read the LAST paragraph of the question first. It usually names the area ("Advise X under contract law…", "applying Contract Law 3…", "under the law of agency…").

Step 2 · The shape of every answer

Each problem = 1–3 ILACs (one per legal issue). Each ILAC has four headings, in this order:

  1. Issue: one question ending in "?", naming the people. "Can Cristina rescind the contract with Patrizia on the basis that Davo lacked authority?"
  2. Law: copy the tab's Law box. Name the case and say in one clause what happened in it.
  3. Application: go through the Law in the same order, using the names and facts. At least once: "As was the case in [case], where [its facts], here [our facts]…" or "Unlike [case]…". If something could go either way, write "On the other hand…".
  4. Conclusion: "On the balance of probabilities, a court would likely find that… Therefore, [person] would be entitled to [remedy]."

The remedy is the last sentence of every Conclusion, which is what the lecturer does in the Week 12 worked answer. Defences only if relevant (e.g. a disclaimer) get their own small ILAC.

There is insufficient information as to [missing fact]; assuming that [assumption], …

Use this whenever a fact you need isn't given. The exam instructions say to point out missing information.

Step 3 · Timing

  • 10 min planning: both questions → Step 1 → list your ILACs for each.
