Ch 1–2 · Probability & Distributions always Q1

Write downOne-liner
🧠\(\int f(x)\,dx = 1\) over the supportEvery "find the constant \(c\)" question starts here — normalise the pdf.
🧠Two unknowns \(\Rightarrow\) two equationsIf the pdf has \(c\) AND \(a\), pair \(\int f = 1\) with the given probability condition (2025 Q1 trick: divide the two integrals to eliminate \(c\)).
🧠\(F(x)=\int_{\min}^{x} f(t)\,dt\); \(P(X\gt a)=1-F(a)\)Build the cdf once, then every probability, the median and quartiles fall out of it.
🧠Median: solve \(F(m)=0.5\) · Mode: maximise \(f\)Mode of a monotone pdf is at the endpoint of the support — no calculus needed.
📄\(\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2\)Always compute variance via the shortcut form — two integrals, one subtraction.
🧠Binomial recognition: fixed \(n\), independent trials, same \(p\)"At least one" \(=1-P(\text{none})\) — the complement is nearly always the fast route.
🧠\(Z=\dfrac{X-\mu}{\sigma}\), then use the Normal tableAny "normally distributed" probability or quantile question is standardise → table.
📄Bayes: \(P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}\), with \(P(B)\) by total probabilityTrigger wording: "given that the test is positive…" — draw the tree first.

Ch 3 · Multiple Random Variables often Q2

Write downOne-liner
🧠Marginal pmf = row/column totals \(\div\; n\)First move on every joint-frequency-table question.
📄\(\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=E[XY]-E[X]E[Y]\); \(\rho=\dfrac{\mathrm{Cov}}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y}\)Compute \(E[XY]\) cell-by-cell from the table, then it's plug-in.
🧠Independent \(\iff f(x,y)=f_X(x)f_Y(y)\) for ALL cellsOne failing cell is a complete proof of dependence.
📄\(\mathrm{Var}(aX+bY)=a^2\mathrm{Var}X+b^2\mathrm{Var}Y+2ab\,\mathrm{Cov}\)Covariance term drops when independent — say so explicitly for the mark.
🧠CLT: \(\bar X \approx N\!\left(\mu,\ \sigma^2/n\right)\) for large \(n\)Trigger: "approximate the probability that the mean/sum exceeds…" — whatever the original distribution.
🧠Estimator checks: unbiased if \(E[\hat\theta]=\theta\); precision = \(\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)\)Q2 estimator-theory parts are exactly these two computations plus a CLT normality statement.

Ch 4–5 · Studies, Data & Descriptives embedded marks

Write downOne-liner
🧠Randomised experiment → causation; observational → association onlyThe word "randomised" is your licence to say "causes" — otherwise never.
🧠Mean & SD are not robust; median & IQR areRight skew pulls mean above median — that comparison IS the skew answer.
📄\(s^2=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2\)Divisor is \(n-1\) — the classic calculator-mode mark-loser.
🧠Outlier fences: \(Q_1 - 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}\), \(Q_3 + 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}\)Points beyond the fences are the boxplot's dots — name them "potential outliers".
🧠Plot-commentary formula: shape · centre · spread · outliers"Comment on the distribution/assumptions using Figure X" = say something about each of the four, in context.

Ch 6 · Estimation & CIs core of Q3

Write downOne-liner
📄\(\bar x \pm t_{n-1,\,\alpha/2}\dfrac{s}{\sqrt n}\)THE workhorse CI — this course uses \(t\) even for large \(n\) (read the \(\infty\) row of the t-table).
🧠"We are 95% confident the population mean [context] lies between L and U."The full-marks interpretation sentence — never say "95% probability".
📄\(\hat p \pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}\)Proportions always use \(z\), never \(t\).
🧠Sample size: \(n=\left(\dfrac{z\,\sigma}{\mathrm{ME}}\right)^2\), round UPTrigger: "how large a sample so the margin of error is at most…" — ceiling, never rounding down.
🧠CI for \(\sigma^2\): \(\left(\dfrac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{\alpha/2}},\ \dfrac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{1-\alpha/2}}\right)\)2024's ambush — "CI for the standard deviation" means chi-squared, not t, and the UPPER quantile makes the LOWER bound (then √ for σ).
🧠Which CI? mean vs proportion → t vs z; one vs two samples; paired → CI on differencesIdentify (data type, #samples, paired?) before touching the sheet.

Ch 7 · Hypothesis Testing core of Q3

Write downOne-liner
🧠The ritual: hypotheses → test+assumptions → statistic → null dist+df → P-value → conclusion in contextEvery "perform an appropriate test" is these six steps, in order, every time.
🧠Template: \(H_0:\mu=\mu_0\) vs \(H_1:\mu\neq\mu_0\), "where \(\mu\) is the population mean [context]""Explain all notation" marks are earned by that where-clause.
🧠Same subjects measured twice → PAIRED → one-sample t on differences"Measured at baseline and again after…" is the tell; the sheet has no paired template — you form \(d_i\) yourself.
📄\(t=\dfrac{\bar x-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt n}\), df \(=n-1\)One-sample and paired tests are the same engine.
🧠Two samples: pooled if SD ratio \(\lt 2\) (course default), else Welch (df \(=\min(m,n)-1\) by hand)Always justify the choice in one sentence — it's a stated mark in recent papers.
🧠P-value from t-table = bracketing: "0.02 \(\lt P \lt\) 0.05" (double the tail if two-sided)You can't read exact P-values off tables — brackets ARE the exam answer.
🧠"At least k more/larger" claims → \(H_0\) with \(\delta_0=k\), one-sidedRecent papers love non-zero nulls — don't autopilot to \(\delta_0=0\).
🧠Type I = reject a true \(H_0\) (prob \(\alpha\)) · Power = P(reject \(H_0\mid H_1\) true)State them in the story's language, not abstractly.

Ch 8 · ANOVA always Q4 PRE-LECTURE

Write downOne-liner
🧠DF rules: factors \(a-1\), \(b-1\); interaction \((a-1)(b-1)\); residual \(N-ab\); total \(N-1\) (one-way: \(k-1,\ N-k\))DF column first — it unlocks every "complete the table" question.
🧠Table algorithm: SS's add to Total · MS = SS/DF · F = MS/MSEThree identities recover every missing X cell (2025 Q4).
🧠Interaction plot: parallel lines → no interaction; crossing/converging → possible interactionThat one sentence, referencing the plot, is the whole 2-mark answer.
🧠Each F-test: \(F=\mathrm{MS}_{\text{effect}}/\mathrm{MSE} \sim F(\mathrm{df}_{\text{eff}},\,\mathrm{df}_{\text{res}})\) under \(H_0\)Quote both df's — "the distribution of the F-statistic under \(H_0\)" is a stated mark.
🧠Assumptions: normality within groups · equal variances · independenceAssess from the given plots/SDs — largest/smallest SD ratio \(\lt 2\) is the quick equal-variance check.
🧠Many t-tests inflate overall Type I error → use Bonferroni multiple comparisonsThe standing conceptual question; "which differ?" = read which Bonferroni CIs exclude 0.
📄\(R^2 = \mathrm{SS}_{\text{model}}/\mathrm{SS}_{\text{total}}\)"Proportion of variability in [response] explained by the model."

Ch 9–10 · Regression & Linear Model always Q5 PRE-LECTURE

Write downOne-liner
🧠Fitted model = Estimate column: \(\hat y = b_0 + b_1 x\)First line of every regression answer — copy the numbers out of the R output.
🧠Slope sentence: "each 1-unit increase in \(x\) changes \(y\) by \(b_1\) on average"Add "holding the other predictors constant" for multiple regression.
🧠Significance: \(t=\mathrm{Estimate}/\mathrm{SE}\), df \(=n-2\); slope CI: \(b_1 \pm t_{n-2}\,\mathrm{SE}(b_1)\)df row of the output gives you \(n\) back: \(n = \mathrm{df}+2\).
🧠CI for the MEAN response vs PI for a NEW observation — PI is always widerAs \(n\to\infty\) the CI shrinks to 0 but the PI floors at \(\pm z\sigma\) (2025's conceptual part).
🧠Residual plot: random cloud = OK; curve → transform (log); funnel → unequal variance; straight Q–Q → normalThe complete diagnostics vocabulary in one line.
🧠Prediction outside the observed \(x\)-range = extrapolation → don't trust it"Would you trust this prediction?" is asking exactly this.
📄\(R^2\): proportion of variability in \(y\) explained by the modelQuote it as a percentage with the response named.

Ch 11 · Chi-squared Tests embedded 5–6 marks PRE-LECTURE

Write downOne-liner
🧠Expected counts: \(E=\dfrac{\text{row total}\times\text{col total}}{n}\)NOT on the sheet — the single most important line in this chapter.
📄\(\chi^2=\sum\dfrac{(O-E)^2}{E}\)Sum over every cell; keep 3+ decimals until the end.
🧠df: independence \((r-1)(c-1)\) · goodness-of-fit \(k-1\) (minus any estimated parameters)The df mix-up is the classic mark-loser between the two test types.
🧠Validity: all expected counts \(\ge 5\)One sentence checking this earns the assumptions mark.
🧠Conclusion: reject/fail to reject + "there is (no) evidence of an association between [X] and [Y]" + directionNaming which cells drive the association (compare O vs E) upgrades a good answer to full marks.

🏆 The 10 lines that pay the most rent

  1. 🧠 The 6-step test ritual — hypotheses → test+assumptions → statistic → null dist+df → P-value → conclusion in context. (Q3, Q4, Q5, every year.)
  2. 📄 \(\bar x \pm t_{n-1,\alpha/2}\, s/\sqrt n\) — the CI workhorse; \(t\) even for large \(n\).
  3. 🧠 "We are 95% confident … lies between L and U" — the only accepted CI sentence.
  4. 🧠 Same subjects twice → paired → t-test on differences — the sheet won't tell you this.
  5. 🧠 \(\int f=1\), then \(F(m)=0.5\) — unlocks Q1 (constants, probabilities, median) every year.
  6. 🧠 ANOVA DF rules + SS additivity + MS=SS/DF + F=MS/MSE — completes any gapped table.
  7. 🧠 \(E=\text{row}\times\text{col}/n\), df \(=(r-1)(c-1)\), all \(E\ge 5\) — the entire embedded χ² part.
  8. 🧠 \(t=\mathrm{Est}/\mathrm{SE}\), df \(=n-2\) — reads significance straight off any R output.
  9. 🧠 CLT: \(\bar X\approx N(\mu,\sigma^2/n)\) — any "probability the mean exceeds…" question.
  10. 🧠 Conclusion template — "Since P \(\lt \alpha\) (or \(\gt\)), we (fail to) reject \(H_0\) at the 5% level; there is (in)sufficient evidence that [claim, in the story's words]."
